                   A TETHER THAT HOLDS NOTHING:
               ENERGY-NULL METRIPLECTIC CORRECTIONS
          AND THE VANISHING ENSTROPHY CONTRIBUTION ON T3

                                       BENJAMIN STANLEY FROHMAN


        Abstract. We record the Frohmanian geometric tether—a symmetric, negative-semidefinite qua-
        dratic correction to the Lie–Poisson bracket on the vorticity coadjoint orbit of ideal incompressible
        fluid—in both its stream-function (ψ) and velocity-slot forms. The velocity-slot kernel Tvel anni-
        hilates the kinetic energy by construction (condition C2), restoring consistency of the metriplectic
        sum with the classical Euler equations. That consistency, however, is precisely the obstruction:
        by symmetry the tether contributes nothing to the enstrophy balance, so no cubic damping of
        enstrophy appears. We state the open analytic hypothesis FrohmanianTetherRate, distinguish its
        inequivalent differential forms, and delimit the scope of the associated product theorem: uncondi-
        tional only for a discontinuous step field, zero data, and constant flow. No claim is made on the
        Clay Millennium problem for the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations.




                                               1. Introduction
   The geometric structure of ideal incompressible fluid on a compact Riemannian manifold is
classical: the Euler equations are Hamiltonian on the coadjoint orbits of the volume-preserving
diffeomorphism group, with the Lie–Poisson bracket of Arnold [Arn66, AK98] and kinetic energy
as Hamiltonian. Dissipative corrections that preserve energy while producing entropy growth—
metriplectic systems in the sense of Morrison [Mor86] and Grmela–Öttinger [GO97]—offer a natural
language in which to ask whether a structurally minimal correction could supply the missing a-priori
bound on vorticity.
   The Frohmanian geometric tether is one such candidate: a symmetric bilinear form T(F, G) on
functionals of vorticity, weighted by global enstrophy and projected orthogonal to the Biot–Savart
velocity. Its design goals are three conditions conventionally labelled C1–C3:
(C1) negative-semidefiniteness of the quadratic form T(F, F );
(C2) annihilation of the kinetic energy, T(H, ·) = 0;
(C3) compatibility with the coadjoint (vorticity) structure.
Condition (C2) ensures that the tethered dynamics leave the ideal Euler equations unchanged when-
ever the dissipative (viscous) term is switched off. The present note records the precise definitions
used in the accompanying Lean development frohmanian-tether-sdiff, the quadratic geometry
of the kernel, a C2 failure of the ψ-slot form, the velocity-slot repair, and the analytic obstruction
that follows once C2 holds.
   The conclusion is negative in a precise sense. The very property that makes the tether con-
sistent with Euler—energy nullity—forces, by symmetry, a vanishing contribution to the enstro-
phy balance. No damping of the form −κ′ M 2 is produced. Closing a differential inequality for
enstrophy along physical Navier–Stokes solutions therefore remains an open analytic step, named

   The tethered bracket {F, G}T = {F, G}LP +T(F, G) is therefore metriplectic rather than Poisson:

an unconditional uniqueness statement.

                       4. The velocity-slot repair and condition C2
4.1. Failure of C2 in the ψ-slot. Condition (C2) asks that T(H, ·) = 0 for the kinetic energy H.
In the ψ-slot form (4) one projects δH/δω = ψ, the stream function. In general ψ ∦ u, so Πu ψ ̸≡ 0.
On 30 October 2026 this was confirmed in Lean on a two-mode shear flow (commit a52c6af): the
ψ-slot kernel fails C2 on that example. The global enstrophy weight, being a scalar factor, cannot
repair a pointwise nonvanishing integrand.

Proposition 4.2 (C2 for the velocity slot). Assume curl(δH/δω) = u = BS(ω). Then Πu u = 0
pointwise, hence Tvel (H, G) = 0 for every functional G.
Proof. By the standing identification of the functional derivative of kinetic energy, δH/δω = ψ with
curl ψ = u. The integrand of (6) at F = H therefore contains the factor Πu u, which vanishes at
every x ∈ T3 by definition of Πu . The scalar prefactor −κ∥ω∥2L2 is immaterial. Thus Tvel (H, G) =
0.                                                                                                □

                        5. The C2 obstruction to enstrophy damping
  Suppose C2 holds in the form T(H, G) = 0 for every second slot G (as it does for Tvel under
the hypotheses of Proposition 4.2). Taking G = M , the enstrophy functional, yields T(H, M ) = 0.
Symmetry of T then gives
                                    T(M, H) = T(H, M ) = 0.                                   (7)
Along the tethered flow the evolution of enstrophy is therefore
                             dM
                                 = {M, H}LP + T(M, H) = {M, H}LP .                               (8)
                              dt
                                  A TETHER THAT HOLDS NOTHING                                        7


The tether contributes nothing. In particular no term of the shape −κ′ M 2 (or −κ′′ M 3 ) is generated
by the geometric correction. The classical stretching contribution {M, H}LP remains exactly as in
the untethered Euler or Navier–Stokes dynamics.
Remark 5.1 (Enstrophy is not a 3D Casimir). The vanishing (7) should not be confused with the
Casimir property. In three dimensions, enstrophy is not a Casimir of the Euler bracket: {M, H}LP
equals the vortex-stretching integral and is nonzero for generic data. The tether simply fails to
modify that classical balance. Replacing “enstrophy” by a true Casimir such as helicity yields the
same formal conclusion—the tether adds nothing—but helicity does not control regularity.
Remark 5.2 (Projection gap). Even an asymmetric tether designed to produce damping would
confront a projection gap. The only estimate available from the definition is the upper bound
                                     Z
                                         |Πu ω|2 dx ≤ ∥ω∥2L2 .
                                          T3
A coercive lower bound on |Πu ω| is what damping requires, and it fails wherever ω ∥ u—precisely
the Beltrami-type regions in which vortex stretching is weakest. No such lower bound follows from
the present geometric construction.
    Thus two routes have been examined.
(1) Inside the bracket (metriplectic tether). The C2/C3 obstruction prevents the symmetric tether
     from producing cubic damping. No modification of the present form removes the obstruction
     while preserving both energy conservation and the coadjoint structure.
(2) Outside the bracket (a-posteriori estimate). Starting from a smooth solution of the unmodified
     3D Navier–Stokes equations, one may form a Lyapunov quantity from its vorticity and attempt
     a differential inequality by classical identities. This avoids the C2 conflict, but the resulting
     inequality does not close for arbitrary data.
 Until an open rate hypothesis is discharged by a genuine estimate, neither route yields global
 regularity for every smooth finite-energy initial datum.

differential inequality of one of the following shapes for its enstrophy (or a supremum-norm proxy)

Remark 6.2 (Inequivalence). The two forms (9) and (10) are not equivalent: they assert different
powers and neither implies the other. A single oﬀicial form must be fixed; referring to both as
“equivalent” is a paper-to-code mismatch. The factor 12 in (1) merely rescales constants and cannot
convert an unproved inequality into a proved one.
   The hypothesis is taken as an explicit argument by the conditional regularity theorems
frohmanian_tether_theorem and global_regularity_for_NS. It is not derived from the classical iden-
tity (2), nor from the geometric tether. Earlier attempts to obtain a pointwise cubic bound of this
shape were withdrawn on grounds of scale inconsistency and the C2 obstruction of Section 5.
   Known obstructions to a direct derivation include:
 • Scale inconsistency. A sink −κM 4 , or −κ′ M 2 with dimensionless κ′ , does not match the para-
   bolic scaling of Navier–Stokes.
 • The C2 obstruction. The symmetric tether contributes zero to the enstrophy balance (8).

 • The projection gap. Only an upper bound on Πu ω is available; cf. Remark 5.2.
 By Beale–Kato–Majda (3), a time-integrated bound on ∥ω∥∞ already yields continuation. The
 differential inequalities (9)–(10) are therefore stronger than necessary; a weaker integrated form
 would suﬀice for the regularity application and may be a more realistic target.
    Concrete next steps that do not claim a solution:
(1) Extract the exact classical stretching integral and the precise Hölder/Sobolev constants already
     present in the Lean files.
(2) Test candidate scale-invariant replacements (for instance −κνM 2 · (dimensionless quantity))
     against known exact solutions and scaling counter-examples.
(3) Record any partial estimate that holds under an extra hypothesis—e.g. a bound on the direction
     of vorticity in the sense of Constantin–Fefferman [CF93]—as a named conditional lemma.

                                  7. Scope of the product theorem
    The Lean declaration
        frohmanian_metriplectic_system_canonical_uniqueness_and_global_-
        regularity_product_theorem
 is assumption-free as a formal object, but its regularity conjunct covers only three regimes:
  (i) a discontinuous step field that is not a Navier–Stokes solution;
 (ii) identically zero data;
(iii) constant (trivial) flow.
 Every theorem in the development that addresses general smooth finite-energy data takes one of the
 named open hypotheses hRiccati, FrohmanianTetherBound, or TetherCoefficientBounded as an explicit
 argument. Consequently:
Theorem 7.1 (Scope; informal). There is no unconditional global-regularity theorem for arbitrary
smooth finite-energy initial data of 3D Navier–Stokes on T3 in the present development. The Clay
Millennium problem [Fef00] is not solved here.
  The honest forward path is therefore to drop the tether from the dynamics and treat any can-
didate bound as an a-posteriori estimate on true Navier–Stokes solutions, labelled explicitly as
conditional on the rate or absorbing-bound hypotheses above.
