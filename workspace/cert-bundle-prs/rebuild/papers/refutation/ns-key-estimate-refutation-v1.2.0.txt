                                                  Abstract
           An earlier unpublished draft by the author claimed global regularity for the 3D incom-
        pressible Navier–Stokes equations via a “symplectic tether”. Its central step was an a-priori
        Riccati estimate M ′ (t) ≤ C − kM (t)2 for the vorticity sup-norm M (t) = ∥ω(·, t)∥L∞ . We
        show that this estimate is false on T3 when C, k depend only on the viscosity ν. We do this
        with an explicit trigonometric initial datum whose peak vorticity strictly increases at t = 0.
        We also show it is false when C, k may depend in an arbitrary way on ν and the kinetic
        energy, or on ν and ∥u0 ∥L3 , by a scaled localized vortex. If the constants may depend on
        the initial datum in an arbitrary way, the estimate is equivalent to global regularity and
        remains open. This note makes no claim about the Clay Millennium Problem.
        A companion Lean 4 audit of the draft proves that its tether form is symmetric and not
        antisymmetric (hence not symplectic), and that because the tether vanishes against the
        energy, B(F, H) = 0, it cannot change the flow. Any vorticity control therefore cannot
        come from the tether. The √ audit also proves that the draft’s tanh solution formula cannot
        match initial data above C/k. It proves the conditional chain “Riccati estimate + BKM
        ⇒ no finite-time blow-up”, with the estimate carried as an explicit hypothesis. Everything
        machine-checked builds without sorry and uses only Lean’s standard axioms.


1       Setting and the estimate
Let T3 = (R/2πZ)3 , ν > 0, and let u be a smooth solution of ∂t u + (u · ∇)u + ∇p = ν∆u,
∇ · u = 0, with vorticity ω = ∇ × u satisfying
                                   ∂t ω = (ω · ∇)u − (u · ∇)ω + ν∆ω.                                      (1)

                         M ′ (t) ≤ C − k M (t)2     on [0, T ),     C ≥ 0, k > 0.                         (2)

2     V1: an explicit counterexample
Let

      U (x, y, z) = 3(− sin y, sin x, 0) + (− sin x cos z, 0, cos x sin z),   u0 = A U, A > 0.

Proposition 2. (Lean: div_free, w1_eq–w3_eq, omega_sq_le, omega_at_origin, omega_
norm_bound, rhs_origin.)
 (a) ∇ · U = 0 and Ω := ∇ × U = (0, 2 sin x sin z, 3(cos x + cos y)).

 (b) |Ω|2 = 9(cos x + cos y)2 + 4 sin2 x sin2 z ≤ 36, with equality at the origin. So M (0) =
     ∥AΩ∥∞ = 6A.

 (c) At x = 0 the right-hand side of (1) for u = AU , ω = AΩ equals (0, 0, 6A(A − ν)).
Proof sketch. (a) and (c) are direct differentiation; at the origin U (0) = 0, the strain is S(0) =
diag(−1, 0, 1), Ω(0) = (0, 0, 6) and ∆Ω(0) = (0, 0, −6). Stretching contributes 6A2 , advection
0 and diffusion −6νA. For (b), (cos x + cos y)2 ≤ 2 cos2 x + 2 and 4 sin2 x sin2 z ≤ 4(1 − cos2 x),
so |Ω|2 ≤ 22 + 14 cos2 x ≤ 36.

Theorem 3 (V1 is false). For every ν > 0, C ≥ 0 and k > 0 there is A > ν (explicitly
A = ν + 1 + |C|/k) such that the smooth solution with u0 = AU violates (2), in either form,
on every interval [0, T ) with T > 0.
Proof. By classical local well-posedness [2, 3], the solution is smooth on [0, T ) × T3 up to
t = 0. Hence g(t) := ω3 (0, t) is C 1 with g ′ (0) = 6A(A − ν) by Proposition 2(c) and (1). Also
g ≤ |ω(0, t)| ≤ M (t) and g(0) = 6A = M (0). (Using the component ω3 avoids differentiating
| · |.) The arithmetic lemma amplitude_exists gives 6A(A − ν) > C − k(6A)2 , and Lemma 1
concludes.

  Concretely, with ν = 1 and A = 3 we get M (0) = 18, and the lower right Dini derivative of
M at 0 is at least 36.


3     V2: constants depending on ν and the energy
Amplitude scaling alone cannot refute V2, since an arbitrary C(E) can absorb the growth of
the energy E. We use Navier–Stokes scaling of a localized field instead.

Theorem 4 (V2 is false). For every ν > 0, E ∗ > 0 and every choice C = C(ν, E ∗ ) ≥ 0,
k = k(ν, E ∗ ) > 0, some smooth divergence-free u0 on T3 with energy exactly E ∗ violates (2) at

the same computation. Constants that may grow with a norm that blows up along the family,
such as ∥u0 ∥H s for s > 21 or ∥ω0 ∥∞ , fall under V3.


4    V3 is equivalent to global regularity, and is open
For a fixed smooth u0 on T3 , the following are equivalent: (a) the solution is global and smooth;
(b) there are C ≥ 0 and k > 0 with D+ M ≤ C − kM 2 for all t ≥ 0.
                                                    √             ∫T
    (b)⇒(a): comparison gives M ≤ max(M (0), C/k), so 0 M < ∞ for every T , and the
Beale–Kato–Majda criterion [1] excludes blow-up.
    (a)⇒(b): the elementary Danskin-type bound D+ M (t) ≤ ∥∂t ω(t)∥∞ holds. For a global
smooth solution, eventual smallness of ∥∇u∥2 , ∫small-data theory and decay of higher norms
[3, 4] (after a Galilean reduction to mean zero if u0 ̸= 0) give supt ∥∂t ω∥∞ < ∞ and supt M <
∞.
    Hence V3 for all smooth data is equivalent to global regularity on T3 , Fefferman’s state-
ment (B) [6] up to the choice of period. It is open, and nothing here proves or disproves
it.
    At a point where |ω| is maximal, ∂t |ω| = |ω| ξ · Sξ + ν ξ · ∆ω with ξ = ω/|ω|. The stretching
term has size ∼ M 2 and can be positive (its value was +1 in V1 and +2 in V2 per unit
amplitude) [5]. Nothing in (1) produces a −kM 2 term.
