4.6   Lemma 4.5 (non-circular majorant) — T5
There exists a scalar Y ∈ C 1 ([0, ∞); [0, ∞)) whose ODE Y 0 = F (Y ) has coeﬃcients independent
of {M (t) : t ≥ 0}, such that M (t) ≤ Y (t) on the existence interval. Scalar ODE comparison,
including a clipped cubic F (y) = Cabs y 2 − κ00 y 3 with a global barrier, is ﬁlled. The missing piece
is the diﬀerential inequality M 0 ≤ F (M ) with an F built only from u0 and ν. That estimate is
the analytic core of the Clay problem. Inserting sup M into Cabs is circular; modifying the PDE is
inadmissible.

4.7   Lemma 4.6 (molliﬁer limit) — T6
Sε (t) → S(t) and the commutators Cε , Rε vanish as ε → 0, with constants compatible with
Lemma 4.5. Passage on a compact classical interval [0, T ] ⊂ [0, T∗ ) is ﬁlled. Using those bounds
to continue past a putative singularity requires constants that remain controlled as T ↑ T∗ . If they
explode with M (t), they cannot close Lemma 4.5.

4.8   Lemma 4.7 (Jacobi / cocycle) — T7
If a bilinear correction B is part of the argument, then d2 B = 0 on the reduced coadjoint orbit.
The algebraic expansion is a ﬁnite computation; the missing piece is veriﬁcation that the particular
kernel cancels in every slot while remaining degenerate on H (Lemma 4.8) and contributing a
                           d
nonzero negative term to dt  M (Lemma 4.2). Those three demands are in tension. If B is dropped
and ϕ is an ordinary weight, this lemma is vacated and Theorem 2.1 is a Lyapunov criterion on
unmodiﬁed Navier–Stokes.

4.9   Lemma 4.8 (degeneracy versus feedback) — T8
A correction used as dynamics satisﬁes B(F, H) = 0. A weight used as a Lyapunov device is
not required to be a Poisson correction. The two readings are not mixed. If B(·, H) = 0, the
Hamiltonian evolution is exactly Euler and no extra term appears in the vorticity equation. If
B(·, H) 6= 0, the evolution is not Navier–Stokes and Theorem 2.1 does not apply. This is ﬁlled as
logic and left open as geometry.

9      Honest scope
• The cubic rate and the uniform vorticity closing bound are open for general smooth, divergence-
  free, ﬁnite-energy (or periodic) data. The repaired rate FrohmanianTetherRateSmooth is like-
  wise open for general Feﬀerman data.

• By Beale–Kato–Majda, a time-uniform kωk∞ bound along every local periodic solution, for all
  such data, is as hard as Statement (B). The gaps T1–T6 of 22835069 are that same missing a
  priori bound under diﬀerent names.

• The formalizations prove implications, not Statement (B), not Feﬀerman’s Problem (A), and
  not the Clay Millennium problem. In particular, clay_A_fefferman_of_frohmanianTether
  RateSmooth would yield Clay_A_Fefferman only after proofs of both of its binders exist.


                                                  14
• R3BKMContinuation (classical continuation on R3 ) remains a hypothesis; it is not the proved
  periodic theorem periodicBKMContinuation_of_classical.

• Known refutations of the physical-class rate and of the old two-sided depletion hypothesis are
  theorems; they are not side remarks.

• Where a repository’s T3 is really R3 , or where a physical-class ﬁlter excludes genuine periodic
  data, a theorem on that class is silent about Feﬀerman’s periodic Statement (B).

• The two-point strain pairing is not free. An L∞ bound on S on the whole torus is emptiness of
  the singular set, not P1 -nullity.
