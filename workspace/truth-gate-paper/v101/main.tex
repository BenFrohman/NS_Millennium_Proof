\begin{abstract}
A green Lean build certifies that the kernel accepted proofs of the propositions written in the
Lean files. It does not certify that those propositions are the ones a paper claims in English.
The \emph{Truth Gate} is a mechanical check that closes part of this gap. Each formalization
repository carries a file \code{Semantics.lean} with two propositions: \code{formal\_claim}, the
statement of the repository's main theorem, and \code{paper\_claim}, an independent Lean
transcription of a verbatim sentence of the paper, recorded with the paper's path, its SHA-256
and its section. A theorem \code{intended\_correspondence : formal\_claim ↔ paper\_claim} must be
proved by real reasoning, with no \code{rfl}/\code{Iff.rfl}, no \code{sorry}, \code{admit} or
\code{native\_decide}, and only the axioms \code{propext}, \code{Classical.choice} and
\code{Quot.sound}. The checker merged into \code{BenFrohman/PipelineHalt} at commit
\code{56e29ec} rejects 15 of 15 locked-and-approved negative fixtures and behaves correctly on
6 of 6 post-approval tamper cases (one passing demo, five rejections) in its own CI. This note
describes the design, the author's rules on partial locks and on reporting solved problems, two
mismatches the gate reports in \code{NS\_Millennium\_Proof}, and a re-audit on 16--17 November
2026 that reopened 11 merged locks as draft pull requests. One of them, the lock in
\code{frohmanian-tether-verified}, passed every mechanical check and was reopened because its
formal claim restates its own hypothesis. A lock certifies that a Lean certificate inhabits the
locked transcription of the paper's claim; whether that claim settles an open problem depends on
what the claim states. This note reports no open problem as solved.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem: drift and the vacuity trap}

A Lean development and the paper that describes it are two documents. The kernel checks the
first; readers read the second. They can diverge in several ways, which this note calls
\emph{drift}: the Lean theorem may be narrower than the paper's sentence, may carry a
hypothesis the paper does not state, may be about a stand-in type, or may be vacuous because one
of its hypotheses cannot be satisfied. In each case every Lean check is green and the paper's
sentence is still unproved.

The obvious repair is to write the paper's claim in Lean and prove it equivalent to the formal
statement. This repair has a trap. If the Lean version of the paper's claim is \emph{defined}
to be the formal statement, or unfolds to it, or is built from the same constants, then the
equivalence is \code{Iff.rfl}. It proves nothing about the paper, and a gate built on it passes
anything. The Truth Gate is designed around this trap.

\section{Design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{The semantics lock}

Every formalization repository contains a module \code{Semantics.lean} with:
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{formal\_claim : Prop}, the statement of the repository's main theorem;
\item \code{paper\_claim : Prop}, an independent, literal Lean transcription of one verbatim
  English claim from the paper. Its documentation records the quote, the paper's path, the
  SHA-256 of that file and the page or section. It is written from the quote and never
  references, unfolds to, or aliases \code{formal\_claim};
\item \code{theorem intended\_correspondence : formal\_claim ↔ paper\_claim}, proved without
  \code{rfl}, \code{Iff.rfl}, \code{⟨id, id⟩} or \code{Iff.of\_eq rfl}, with zero
  \code{sorry}, \code{admit} or \code{native\_decide}, and with \code{\#print axioms} showing
  only \code{propext}, \code{Classical.choice} and \code{Quot.sound};
\item a public theorem of type \code{paper\_claim}, obtained as
  \code{intended\_correspondence.mp} applied to the proof of \code{formal\_claim}.
\end{itemize}
Conditional results keep every hypothesis explicit on both sides, in the form
$H_1\to\dots\to\text{Conclusion}$; a conditional result is never stated as its bare conclusion.
A lock file fixes both statements by hash, together with the paper hash and, in the reusable
harness, the toolchain and the approver. Changing a locked statement requires the author's
explicit approval and a new lock (\code{TRUTH\_GATE.md} in PipelineHalt at \code{56e29ec},
rules 1--6).

\subsection{What the checker rejects}

The reusable checker is \code{scripts/verify-lock.sh} in \code{BenFrohman/PipelineHalt},
merged by PR~\#4 (head \code{bd11758}, merge commit
\code{56e29ecaf9b1616eaa327543d7e357d8f5d86fd8}, merged 2026-11-17 01:06:59 UTC). It runs six
stages and prints either \code{TRUTH GATE: PASS} or \code{TRUTH GATE: REJECT <reason>}:
\begin{enumerate}[start=0]
\item preflight: the lock, \code{Semantics.lean} and \code{intended\_correspondence} exist;
\item compile only the locked Semantics module;
\item an \emph{observation pass}, the first Lean step, which changes nothing:
  \code{\#check intended\_correspondence}, \code{\#print paper\_claim},
  \code{\#print formal\_claim}, \code{\#print axioms};
\item a full \code{lake build}, which includes an in-build \code{\#eval} lock audit so that the
  ordinary build fails when the lock is broken;
\item \code{lake env lean scripts/SemanticsLock.lean};
\item \code{pipelinehalt truth-gate check --require-observation}, which evaluates the lock
  integrity checks (a.*), the correspondence checks (b.*) and the transport checks (c.*).
\end{enumerate}
Mathlib is never rebuilt; its compiled files are fetched with \code{lake exe cache get}.

\subsection{Drift in CI and author approval}

Each repository calls the gate on push and pull request to \code{main}, so a change that breaks
the lock turns CI red. The lock carries an approval record: the check \code{a.approval}
(\code{src/truthgate.ts} at \code{56e29ec}) passes only when the lock has been approved by the
author and the approved digest equals the current digest of the lock fields; otherwise it
reports that a new approval is required. In \code{NS\_Millennium\_Proof} PR~\#34 (open, head
\code{5856fa9}) the workflow has a separate job, \emph{Lock approved by the author}, which runs
\code{SKIP\_BUILD=1 REQUIRE\_APPROVAL=1 bash scripts/verify-lock.sh}; it is red on that PR
because the transcriptions there have not been approved.

\subsection{No partial locks}

The author's rule, issued on 16 November 2026 for all agents and collaborators, is that a lock
certifies only a step on the correct path that is kernel-proved as the true answer, so that the
next step can build from it. Drift is rejected, and so are dummy or stand-in types and names.
Connections across ontologies (for example, between a geometric and an analytic formulation)
are allowed; they are judged only by the kernel check and by whether they help reach the full
proof. In the commits cited here this rule is a written policy enforced by review; it is not a
separate mechanical check of PipelineHalt. Section~\ref{sec:reaudit} reports the re-audit of the
merged locks against it.

\subsection{Reporting solved problems}
\label{sec:solved}

On 16 November 2026 at 7:49 PM (US Central Time) the author set the following rule for every
agent working on his formalizations, quoted verbatim:
\begin{quote}\small
``Always tell user immediately when an OPEN problem is SOLVED regardless if it is the open
problem i am working on or another that happens to be resolved by proxy of nearby findings or by
direct aim or by side tangental work that brings about other findings.''
\end{quote}
With it:
\begin{itemize}
\item When a problem the author is working on is solved, in Lean or on paper, the agent stops at
  that point and states honestly that the problem is closed.
\item ``Solved'' means honestly proved: kernel-checked under every gate rule, that is, an
  independent \code{paper\_claim}, no aliasing and no \code{rfl} correspondence, only the standard
  axioms, no \code{sorry}, and no partial lock.
\item A lock certifies that the Lean development matches the paper's locked claim. Whether that
  claim settles an open problem depends on what the claim states. A conditional lock certifies
  an implication and stays conditional.
\end{itemize}
No lock described in this note states that an open problem is solved.

\section{Case study: a lock that passed the checker and was reopened}
\label{sec:example}

This section shows a complete lock as it was merged, and then why the re-audit reopened it. It is
not an example of a sound lock.


The lock was merged by PR~\#6 (merge commit
\code{9acd434cd70a732c6b4c5f4616ac3f48eb640382}, 2026-11-16 20:25 UTC) and hardened by PR~\#7
(merge commit \code{fa82d34a15a0c61d2ba24167517896ab5276c52d}, 2026-11-16 21:25 UTC). PR~\#7
adds anti-alias and anti-\code{rfl} checks and a \code{PAPER\_SECTION} field; the lock file
\code{TRUTH\_GATE.lock} is byte-identical at both commits. This repository uses the per-repository
form of the gate (\code{TRUTH\_GATE.lock}, \code{scripts/truth\_gate\_check.py},
\code{scripts/TruthGateAudit.lean}, \code{scripts/verify-lock.sh}), which predates the reusable
PipelineHalt harness of Section~\ref{sec:design} and enforces the same core rules.

The locked sentence is in \code{paper/frohmanian-tether-verified.tex}, whose SHA-256 is
\code{93bde1b66ac8ee68e33935c77c95a7a76e7a1bd5badd9b66f6663f110886cef9} (recomputed for this
note from the file at \code{fa82d34}), Section~5 ``Refuted claims'', first item:
\begin{quote}\small
The tether damps the energy budget. This is refuted. C2 forces $B(M,H)=0$, so the tether
contributes nothing to $dH/dt$
\end{quote}
The lock region of \code{FTVerified/Semantics.lean} at \code{fa82d34}:
\begin{Verbatim}
def formal_claim : Prop :=
  ∀ (B : CoadjointOrbit → Functional → Functional → ℝ),
    DegenerateWRTKineticEnergy B → ∀ (ω : CoadjointOrbit) (M : Functional),
      B ω M KineticEnergyHamiltonian = 0

def paper_claim : Prop :=
  ∀ (B : CoadjointOrbit → Functional → Functional → ℝ),
    DegenerateWRTKineticEnergy B → ∀ ω : CoadjointOrbit,
      (∀ M : Functional, B ω M KineticEnergyHamiltonian = 0) ∧
        B ω KineticEnergyHamiltonian KineticEnergyHamiltonian = 0

theorem intended_correspondence : formal_claim ↔ paper_claim := by
  constructor
  · intro h B hB ω
    exact ⟨fun M => h B hB ω M, h B hB ω KineticEnergyHamiltonian⟩
  · intro h B hB ω M
    exact (h B hB ω).1 M
\end{Verbatim}
followed, outside the lock region, by
\begin{Verbatim}
theorem formal_claim_holds : formal_claim :=
  fun B hB ω M => ObstructionAudit.flow_contribution_zero_of_C2 B hB ω M

theorem paper_claim_holds : paper_claim :=
  intended_correspondence.mp formal_claim_holds
\end{Verbatim}
The two sides are not the same proposition: \code{paper\_claim} adds the clause that the tether
term in $dH/dt$, namely $B(\omega,H,H)$, vanishes, which is how the documentation fixes the
reading of ``contributes nothing to $dH/dt$''. The correspondence is a statement-level
equivalence that does not use the main theorem. \code{TruthGateAudit.lean} checks this in the
kernel environment: it fails if \code{paper\_claim} mentions \code{formal\_claim}, if the two
bodies are syntactically equal or definitionally equal, if the correspondence reaches the main
theorem or the \code{*\_holds} lemmas, if \code{formal\_claim\_holds} does not use the main
theorem, or if \code{paper\_claim\_holds} does not go through the correspondence.

The \code{truth-gate} workflow run 43959965556 on \code{fa82d34} (push, success) printed:

build: no sorry outside draft dirs

Run 43949622534 on \code{9acd434} printed the same audit lines and
\code{TRUTH GATE PASS at 9acd434cd70a732c6b4c5f4616ac3f48eb640382}.

\code{TRUTH\_GATE.lock} at \code{fa82d34} contains no approval field; the author's sign-off on
this transcription is not recorded in the lock file.

\paragraph{Why it was reopened.} Draft PR~\#8
(\url{https://github.com/BenFrohman/frohmanian-tether-verified/pull/8}, head \code{8e06646},
opened 2026-11-17 01:42 UTC, unmerged) sets \code{"certifying": false} in \code{TRUTH\_GATE.lock}
and makes the gate red on purpose (truth-gate run 43997671203: failure). The hypothesis (C2) is
defined in \code{FTVerified/Sdiff/SymplecticTether.lean} as
\begin{Verbatim}
def DegenerateWRTKineticEnergy (B : ...) : Prop :=
  ∀ (F : Functional) (ω : CoadjointOrbit), B ω F KineticEnergyHamiltonian = 0
\end{Verbatim}
and the locked main theorem in \code{FTVerified/Sdiff/ObstructionAudit.lean} is
\begin{Verbatim}
theorem flow_contribution_zero_of_C2 (B : ...) (hC2 : DegenerateWRTKineticEnergy B)
    (ω : CoadjointOrbit) (M : Functional) : B ω M KineticEnergyHamiltonian = 0 :=
  hC2 M ω
\end{Verbatim}
The PR gives three reasons: the theorem restates its own hypothesis, so the lock certifies nothing
beyond the definition (a tautology, not a step); the quoted sentence is one item of the paper's
list of refuted claims, which prunes a path rather than giving a step the next step can build on;
and \code{CoadjointOrbit} is the type of all divergence-free fields with an abstract
\code{SDiff} action, a stand-in name. On \code{main} the lock is unchanged and its CI is still
green, because PR~\#8 is a draft.

\paragraph{What this shows about the checker.} The mechanical checks verify that
\code{paper\_claim} is independent of \code{formal\_claim}, that the two are not definitionally
equal, that the correspondence is proved without the main theorem, and that only standard axioms
are used. All of these held here. They do not detect that \code{formal\_claim} is true by the
definition of its own hypothesis, that the quoted sentence is not a step toward the paper's
result, or that a type name overstates what the type is. Those judgements were made by review
under the no-partial-locks rule.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item The gate certifies that the kernel accepted a proof of the locked \code{paper\_claim}.
  Whether that settles an open problem depends on what the locked claim states; no lock in this
  note states that an open problem is solved (Section~\ref{sec:solved}).
\item The mechanical checks do not detect a formal claim that is true by the definition of its
  hypothesis, a claim that is not a step toward the paper's result, or a stand-in name
  (Section~\ref{sec:example}). These are caught by review.
\item The fidelity of a transcription from English to Lean is not machine-checkable. It rests
  on the author's approval or an independent reviewer. The gate checks that the quote occurs in
  the hashed paper and that the transcription is independent of the formal side; it cannot
  check that the transcription is the right reading.
\item Conditional results stay conditional. A lock on $H\to C$ certifies the implication, not $C$.
\item The definitional-equality test in the per-repository audit has a heartbeat cap; a timeout
  is reported as inconclusive and the syntactic and transport checks still apply.
\item Two implementations exist at the cited commits: the per-repository scripts used in the
  three merged locks above and the reusable PipelineHalt harness. The rollout to the remaining
  repositories uses the latter.
\item The no-partial-locks rule is enforced by review, not by a separate mechanical check. The
  re-audit reopened 11 locks (Section~\ref{sec:reaudit}); its verdict on the remaining merged
  locks is not published.
\end{itemize}
