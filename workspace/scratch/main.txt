                                             Abstract
       We describe a Lean 4/Mathlib formalization
                                                R      of the Frohmanian tether, a symmetric
   quadratic correction T (F, G) = −κ kωk2L2 hΠu δF /δω, Πu δG/δωi added to the Arnold
   Lie–Poisson bracket of ideal fluids, where Πu is the L2 -orthogonal projection away from
   the velocity u and κ = 3/2. The library proves: the negative-feedback condition (C3)
   unconditionally; a uniqueness (“canonicity”) statement inside a restricted kernel class;
   and an obstruction to damping: every symmetric kernel B satisfying the kinetic-energy
   degeneracy (C2) has B(H, M ) = B(M, H) = 0 for every functional M , so such a kernel
   contributes no damping to any functional, in particular not to kωkL∞ . (C2) is not proved
   for T . (C1) with the functionals held fixed is refuted for T under a concrete group of
   measure-preserving maps, using a translation; the transported (equivariant) form of (C1),
   in which the functionals move with the vorticity, T (g · ω; F ◦ g −1 , G ◦ g −1 ) = T (ω; F, G),
   is formalized and proved for T under all translations (via translation equivariance of the
   functional gradient and of Biot–Savart); for rotations and general volume-preserving diffeo-
   morphisms it is open. Using a new family of smooth, compactly supported, divergence-free
   test fields ∇ × (χe2 ) concentrated at any point with any radius, the library proves that T
   satisfies the corrected weight condition of its canonicity theorem (every locally integrable
   density of T equals −κkωk2 almost everywhere). Version 0.1.2 removes every hypothesis
   of earlier versions that turned out to be false or vacuous, with Lean refutations where
   possible: δH/δω = u, transitivity of the action on Casimir levels, the pointwise strain
   bound k∇u(x)k ≤ CkωkL∞ , the invariance hypothesis TetherIntegralPreserved, a
   circular Jacobi lemma, an antisymmetric uniqueness clause that can hold only for T ≡ 0,
   and a uniform lower bound on local existence times; it also repairs the definition of a
   physical solution, which in earlier versions admitted only solutions constant in time. A
   further pass rebuilds the functional derivative as the unique divergence-free L2 gradient
   along smooth compactly supported test fields (so it is a genuine, choice-free derivative),
   proves that T is nondegenerate, refutes the unrestricted polarization identity for T and the
   pointwise weight hypothesis of the canonicity theorem, refutes for T the almost-everywhere
   weight hypothesis over all (possibly non-measurable) densities and restricts it to locally
   integrable densities, and restricts the Kato local-existence witness to its own time interval,
   which makes the continuation from local to global solutions an explicit hypothesis. On
   the analytic side the library proves a scalar comparison lemma for y ′ ≤ Cy 2 − κ′′ y 3
   and several conditional implications of the form “(cubic rate or uniform vorticity bound
   on local solutions) + (Kato local existence) + (Kato–Beale–Kato–Majda continuation)
   + (uniqueness) ⇒ global smooth solution”, in which every analytic input is an explicit
   hypothesis. The rate and closing-bound hypotheses are open, are not supplied by the

1     Introduction and scope
Arnold’s geometric picture of ideal hydrodynamics [1, 2] describes the Euler equations as Hamil-
tonian motion on coadjoint orbits of the R group SDiff of volume-preserving diffeomorphisms,
with Lie–Poisson
           R      bracket {F, G}cl (ω) = ω · (δF /δω × δG/δω) and kinetic-energy Hamiltonian
H(ω) = 21 |BS ω|2 . The Frohmanian tether programme adds to this bracket a symmetric,
negative-semidefinite quadratic term T and asks whether T can supply a damping mechanism
for the vorticity sup-norm M (t) = kω(t)kL∞ , which by the Beale–Kato–Majda criterion [3]
controls continuation of smooth Navier–Stokes solutions.
    This paper reports exactly what the accompanying Lean 4 library proves. The Lean state-
ments are the source of truth: every result below is stated to match a checked Lean declaration,
with every hypothesis made explicit, and the exact Lean text is reproduced in Appendix A. The
main findings are:
    (i) The tether T satisfies (C3) unconditionally (Section 3). (C2) is not proved. (C1) with fixed
        functionals is refuted for T ; the transported form of (C1) is proved for T under translations
        (Section 3.1). The identification δH/δω = u assumed for (C2) in earlier versions is false
        with the library’s definitions; this is proved in Lean (Theorem 3.6). The hypotheses
        formerly used for (C1) were false or unprovable and have been removed (Section 3.3).
 (ii) Obstruction to damping. For every symmetric kernel B satisfying (C2), B(H, M ) = 0
      for every functional M ; in particular, if T satisfied (C2), then T (M, H) = T (H, M ) = 0
      (Section 4). Either way, the symmetric design cannot both preserve the Euler vector field
      and produce the cubic damping rate on which the programme relied.
(iii) Canonicity holds only inside a restricted class and follows from saturation and polariza-
      tion hypotheses; conditions (C1)–(C3) appear in the statement but are not used (Sec-
      tion 5). For T itself, nondegeneracy, polarization on differentiable functionals, saturation,
      projection-dependence and the canonical density are proved; unrestricted polarization and
      the pointwise weight hypothesis are refuted; the corrected a.e. weight condition is proved
      (Section 6).
 (iv) The analytic layer consists of proved real-variable lemmas and conditional implications.
      Their remaining hypotheses are of two kinds (Section 11.1): classical theorems that are
      not in Mathlib (Kato local existence, Beale–Kato–Majda continuation, uniqueness), and
      hypotheses that are as strong as the open problem itself (a cubic rate or a uniform vorticity
      bound for all data) (Sections 8–9).
 (v) Explicit solutions (Section 10). The decaying shear and ABC flows are proved to be
     nonzero exact solutions of the library’s equations and, for k = 2πm, global smooth periodic
     solutions for their own data. No nonzero periodic field is a Kato witness (infinite energy
     on R3 ), so the R3 chain cannot be instantiated on them; this is proved, not assumed.

Sanity. The property is not vacuous: the point-evaluation kernel B(ω; F, G) = |ω(e0 /2)| fails
it (not_transported_invariant_pointEval). It differs from the fixed-functional (C1): on the
same translation subgroup the latter fails for T (not_tetherKernel_invariant_transSubgroup,
via the generic not_tetherKernel_invariant_of_shift), while the transported form holds.

Open: rotations and general diffeomorphisms. The concrete group acts by scalar com-
position ω ◦ g −1 , which is the correct transport of a vorticity only for translations; for a rotation
R the geometric action is R ω(R−1 x) and for a volume-preserving diffeomorphism φ it is the
push-forward (Dφ ω) ◦ φ−1 . Neither action is formalized, and transport-invariance of T under
them is open. Under the full concrete group (which contains non-smooth measure-preserving
maps) one expects transport-invariance to fail, since such maps destroy regularity and hence
gradients.
