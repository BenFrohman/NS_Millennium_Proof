                                                 Abstract
         We collect, in a self-contained form, the three classical pillars of the local and condi-
     tional theory of the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations on the torus
     T3 . Pillar I is Kato’s local existence theorem together with an explicit lifespan bound for
     arbitrary divergence-free data in H s (s > 5/2). Pillar II is the Fujita–Kato theorem of
     global regularity for suﬀiciently small data in the critical space Ḣ 1/2 . Pillar III is the Beale–
     Kato–Majda continuation criterion in its one-directional form: if the vorticity (or strain)
     supremum is integrable on [0, T ], then a maximal Kato solution remains smooth up to time
     T . These results form the standard rigorous baseline; the question of unconditional global
     regularity for arbitrary large smooth data (Clay Millennium Problem Statement B) remains
     open.


1   Introduction
The three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations on the torus T3 = R3 /(2πZ)3
read
               ∂t u + (u · ∇)u + ∇p = ν∆u,      ∇ · u = 0,    u(·, 0) = u0 ,          (1)
with ν > 0. Taking the curl yields the vorticity formulation

                        ∂t ω + (u · ∇)ω = (ω · ∇)u + ν∆ω,               ∇ · ω = 0.                          (2)

The classical theory of these equations rests on three fundamental results, established between
1964 and 1984. We record them here as a compact reference, with proofs expanded to the
level of standard energy and commutator estimates. No claim is made that the large-data
global-regularity problem is solved.

5   Concluding remarks and status matrix
The three theorems together constitute the classical baseline of the local and conditional theory.
Pillar I guarantees a unique smooth solution for a short time for every reasonable initial datum.
Pillar II shows that the solution is in fact global when the datum is small in the critical norm.
Pillar III gives a precise one-directional obstruction to continuation for large data: the time-
integrability of the vorticity (or strain) supremum. Whether this obstruction can ever occur for
smooth solutions of the Navier–Stokes equations remains the open Clay Millennium Problem.
