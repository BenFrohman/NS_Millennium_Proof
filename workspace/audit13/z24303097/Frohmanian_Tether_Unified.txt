 GEOMETRIC PHASE-SPACE COMPLETION AND CONDITIONAL GLOBAL
    REGULARITY FOR THE 3D INCOMPRESSIBLE NAVIER–STOKES
                        EQUATIONS

                                       BENJAMIN STANLEY FROHMAN


       Abstract. Let u0 ∈ C ∞ (T3 ; R3 ) be a smooth, divergence-free initial velocity field on the flat
       three-dimensional torus T3 = R3 /(2πZ)3 . We formulate a geometric completion of Arnold’s Lie–
       Poisson framework on SDiﬀ(T3 ) via a three-legged metriplectic architecture: (1) the skew-symmetric
       Arnold Lie–Poisson bracket governing ideal transport, (2) a symmetric metriplectic dissipation sec-
       tor incorporating viscous damping alongside a degenerate projected metric correction B(F, G) (the
       Frohmanian Symplectic Tether), and (3) Marsden–Weinstein–Ratiu coadjoint orbit reduction. Be-
       cause B(F, H) ≡ 0 for the kinetic-energy Hamiltonian H, the generated dynamics recover the exact,
       unmodified 3D Navier–Stokes vorticity transport equation. Within this framework we record three
       rigorous regularity results: a sharp conditional global continuation theorem via critical L1t L∞ x full-
       gradient and strain integrability (Theorem A: Forms B-grad and B-strain), unconditional local-in-
       time smoothness on the deterministic Kato lifespan (Theorem B), and unconditional global smooth-
       ness for small critical Ḣ 1/2 data (Theorem C). Finally, we isolate the supercritical L2t L2x → L1t L∞
                                                                                                             x
       scaling gap as the precise open frontier for geometric vorticity-coherence analysis. No unconditional
       large-data claim is made; Clay Statement (B) remains open.

        1. Statement of the Problem: The Clay Millennium Problem and the
                              Supercriticality Barrier
1.1. Classical PDE Posing (Fefferman Formulation). Let T3 = R3 /(2πZ)3 denote the flat
three-dimensional torus. The 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes equations in velocity-pressure form
are                            

                               ∂t u + (u · ∇)u + ∇p = ν∆u,
                                 div u = 0,                                                 (1.1)
                               
                               
                                 u(x, 0) = u0 (x),
where u0 ∈ C ∞ (T3 ; R3 ) is smooth and divergence-free, p is the scalar pressure, and ν > 0 is
the kinematic viscosity. Taking the curl of (1.1) eliminates the pressure gradient and yields the
equivalent vorticity transport equation for ω = curl u:
                           ∂t ω + (u · ∇)ω = (ω · ∇)u + ν∆ω,                 div ω = 0.                           (1.2)
The Clay Millennium Prize Problem (Statement B) asks whether, for every smooth divergence-free
u0 , there exists a unique global classical solution u ∈ C ∞ (T3 × [0, ∞); R3 ) (T ∗ = ∞), or whether
a finite-time singularity (T ∗ < ∞) can form [5].

Statement (B), the BKM criterion, smooth-implies-bounded-strain, and the differential inequality)
remain assumed structure fields; the Lean development does not claim an unconditional resolution
of the Millennium problem.

4.1. Isolation of the L2t L2x → L1t L∞ x Scaling Gap. As demonstrated by Theorems 3.1–3.4,
the large-data global regularity problem reduces to establishing a data-only a priori bound on
∫T                    ∫T
 0 ∥∇u(t)∥L dt or 0 ∥S(t)∥L dt (Forms B-grad / B-strain). The phase-space geometry makes
             ∞                  ∞

transparent why standard energy methods cannot bridge this gap:
    (1) Supercritical Dissipation vs. Critical Strain. The only coercive global bound gener-
        ated unconditionally by [F, H]diss is the L2t L2x enstrophy dissipation (1.3), which scales as
        λ−1 under parabolic rescaling. Forms B-grad / B-strain require L1t L∞               0
                                                                               x control (λ ).
    (2) Necessity of Viscous Coupling. Because B(F, H) ≡ 0 leaves the reversible Euler sector
        untouched—and inviscid 3D Euler admits finite-time singularities in low-regularity classes—
        no purely symplectic orbit invariant can close Form B-grad / B-strain without exploiting
        viscous dissipation.
4.2. Directions for Geometric-Coherence Analysis. While the current degenerate bracket
B(F, G) preserves the unmodified flow without injecting artificial damping, its projected structure
Πu points toward geometric regularity criteria. Expressing the strain via the Constantin–Fefferman
singular integral kernel [4] shows that vortex stretching cancels when the vorticity direction ξ =
ω/|ω| remains Lipschitz-coherent in intense regions. Whether localized coherence bounds on ξ can
be coupled to [F, G]diss to bridge the scaling gap for arbitrary large data remains open.
