Benjamin Stanley Frohman builds exact, checkable pieces of modern mathematics. This short learning film highlights three of his strongest contributions — chosen for substance, honesty about what is proved, and reproducibility — and teaches the mechanism of each one in plain language.

First: a number-theory jump of length twelve. Given a whole number n, ask whether Euler's totient of n plus twelve equals the totient of n, plus twelve, and whether the sum-of-divisors function makes exactly the same jump.

Known composite solutions come from prime quadruplets, giving n equals p times p plus eight. Benjamin treats the next open family: n equals a squared prime times a different prime q. In his paper he proves a closed-shape theorem ruling out many forms of the shifted number N equals n plus twelve.

Most sharply, his paper proves N cannot be a product of three distinct primes — Theorem twenty-eight. Whether any p-squared-q solution exists at all remains Conjecture three, still open. Finite computer searches find none so far; that is evidence, not the proof of the conjecture.

Second: geometric structure for fluids. Ideal incompressible motion is Hamiltonian on Arnold's Lie–Poisson bracket. Benjamin introduces a Frohmanian tether: a symmetric correction designed to drain excess vorticity while leaving kinetic energy unchanged.

Lean and SymPy verify that, for this tethered sum, bracketing a function with itself equals the tether term and need not be zero — so in his sense the sum is metriplectic, not Poisson. For this same tether, forcing energy preservation also cancels its contribution to enstrophy. That obstruction is specific to this construction, not a claim about every energy-preserving dissipation. An analytic rate hypothesis remains open.

Third: classical Navier–Stokes on the three-torus, formalized in Lean. Local existence, energy uniqueness, and the Beale–Kato–Majda criterion appear as theorems about smooth periodic solutions of the real equations — classical analysis, machine-checked with the standard axioms. This does not invent those classical theorems; it encodes them.

Clay Statement B still reduces to one named open hypothesis: vorticity integrability. The formal ladder is built; the top rung is labeled missing. Three lessons: a paper theorem on shapes, a tether-specific obstruction, and a checkable classical chain. Sources are on screen.
