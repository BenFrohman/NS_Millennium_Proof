                 Conditional regularity criterion with filled classical lemmas
                      and named obstructions on the remaining slots

                                               Abstract
          The official proposed theorem of this redraft is a conditional regularity criterion on the
      exact three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations. The Clay Mathematics In-
      stitute problem on Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness is the problem of global regular-
      ity for that system. They are one statement, not two enigmas. Classical lemmas (transport
      cancellation, viscous dissipation, algebraic stretching identity, Young at κ = 3/2, Hölder,
      Sobolev embedding on T3 , and the published Kato and Beale–Kato–Majda theorems) are
      proved or cited with complete proofs. The lemmas that would convert the criterion into the
      Clay statement—a genuine strain endpoint, stretching identification with the angular kernel
      and vanishing commutators, a majorant whose coefficients depend only on initial data, and
      the mollifier limit—are expanded to the obstruction and then stopped. Chat fragments that
      contradict the exact PDE or reintroduce κ = 1 are not used.

1     Clay statement and global regularity are the same problem
Fefferman’s official formulation asks whether, for every smooth divergence-free initial velocity
of finite energy, the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations admit a unique
solution that remains smooth for all positive times. That is precisely global regularity of the
Cauchy problem. There is no separate “enigmatic process of incompressible flow” whose res-
olution would produce global regularity as an accidental by-product, and no separate global-
regularity enigma whose resolution would produce Navier–Stokes smoothness as a by-product.
A proof of one is a proof of the other. A criterion that assumes a bound in order to conclude a
bound is neither.
     The official proposed theorem below is therefore not a second problem. It is the Clay state-
ment with its unfinished hypotheses written as named lemmas. Filling those hypotheses with
first-principles, non-circular, solution-independent estimates would be the Clay proof. Declaring
them filled because the names coincide is circular.

5    What chat fragments contribute, and what they do not
The following fragments from prior conversations are used.

• The ruling that a metriplectic extra damping term changes the PDE and is excluded. That
  is incorporated as the exact system (1)–(2).

• The ruling that κ = 1 is illegitimate in three dimensions. That is Lemma 3.4.

• The ruling that an a-posteriori Lyapunov functional on the exact equations is a criterion,
  not a completed Clay proof. That is the official status of Theorem 2.1.

• Route A: geometric claims, if any, are separated from the analytic criterion. That is Lemma 4.8.

    The following fragments are not used.

• Any construction in which B is simultaneously degenerate on H and the source of a new
  negative term in the vorticity equation.

• Any estimate that inserts supt<T M (t) into Cabs or into the existence time of the majorant.

• Any identification of C∗ with 3/2 on L∞ .

• Any claim that filling classical lemmas (transport, viscosity, Kato, BKM, Young) constitutes
  global regularity.


6    What would convert Theorem 2.1 into the Clay theorem
Exactly one additional ingredient is required: a proof of Lemmas 4.1, 4.2, 4.4, 4.5, and 4.6 with
constants depending only on ∥u0 ∥H s and ν, for arbitrary smooth divergence-free u0 . Those five
lemmas are the same missing a priori bound under different names. Proving them would prove
Theorem 2.1 unconditionally, which is the Clay statement, which is global regularity.
   No rearrangement of those names, and no observation that incompressible flow and global
regularity share an “enigma,” discharges the estimate.
