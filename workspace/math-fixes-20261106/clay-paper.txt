                                             Abstract
         This monograph records, in Lean 4 against Mathlib, a precise formal statement of
      Feﬀerman’s Statement (B) on global smoothness of the unforced incompressible Navier–
      Stokes equations on T3 = R3 /Z3 , together with an exact ledger of the analytic inputs
      that the Frohmanian symplectic tether does not supply. Statement (B) is not proved
      here. What is proved is a reduction: Statement (B) follows from the classical local theory
      with the Beale–Kato–Majda continuation criterion and from the open hypothesis that
      the Beale–Kato–Majda integral is ﬁnite on every ﬁnite interval of existence. We also
      record the two-dimensional clock test: constant vortex direction (sin φ ≡ 0) coexists
      with linear growth ∥ϕ(t)∥L∞ = M02 t on steady Euler equilibria.


1     Scope and non-claim boundary
This repository is a boundary and gap ledger. It is not a resolution of the Clay Millennium
Problem. Every open input is a named proposition in Lean; none is assumed as an axiom,
and no proof uses sorry. The build and axiom audit (lean/AxiomReport.lean, output
in AXIOMS.md) reports only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. The active
continuation of this programme, in which the remaining gap is decomposed into ﬁner named
statements (all open or conditional, apart from individually proved lemmas such as the planar
sinShear obstruction; the open statement is the Frohmanian Tether Hypothesis (A)/(B),
see NAMING.md), is BenFrohman/periodic-ns-classical-continuation.

2     Statement (B)
Following the oﬃcial problem description of Feﬀerman [1], ﬁx ν > 0 and let u◦ be a smooth,
divergence-free vector ﬁeld on R3 with u◦ (x + ej ) = u◦ (x) for j = 1, 2, 3. Take f ≡ 0.

      Statement (B). There exist smooth functions p(x, t), ui (x, t) on R3 × [0, ∞)
      such that
                        ∑
                        3
              ∂t ui +         uj ∂j ui = ν ∆ui − ∂i p,       div u = 0,   u(x, 0) = u◦ (x),
                        j=1

      and u, p are 1-periodic in each spatial coordinate.

    In Lean this is the proposition Clay.StatementB.FeffermanStatementB (ﬁle lean/Cla
y/StatementB.lean). Space is R3 realised as Euclidean space on Fin 3; partial derivatives
are Fréchet derivatives along the standard basis; the time derivative is taken within [0, ∞);
smoothness is C ∞ on [0, ∞) × R3 . No component of the statement is opaque or trivial.

Theorem 1 (feffermanStatementB_of_bkm). PeriodicMaximalSolutionWithBKM and
PeriodicBKMIntegrability together imply Statement (B).  [Proved as an implication;
Conditional on both hypotheses]

Theorem 2 (bkmIntegralFinite_of_holographic). If ∥ω(t)∥L∞ is bounded on every

   The second hypothesis of the ﬁrst theorem is open and is equivalent in strength to
Statement (B) itself. Consequently the reduction does not prove Statement (B).

The axiom report lists 62 theorem declarations (including compiler-generated auxiliary lem-

the two-dimensional clock results steady_euler_clock, alignment_does_not_delete_clock,
clock_fails_on_aligned_2d_solutions, the planar-vorticity lemma curl_planar, the
tether identities tether_degeneracy, tether_bracket_preservation, and the integer
rearrangement conditional_riccati. This is a machine check of the Lean statements, not
peer review.
    Conditional on. The global-existence reduction takes PeriodicMaximalSolutionW
ithBKM (classical local theory and continuation, not proved here) and PeriodicBKMInteg
rability (the still-undischarged BKM integrability assumption) as arguments. Neither is
supplied by the reduction.
    The separate declaration conditional_riccati is an algebraic implication over integers.
For rate, C, κ, M ∈ Z, CorrectedMaxRate is exactly

                                    rate ≤ M (CM − κM 2 ),

and the theorem rewrites its right-hand side as CM 2 −κM 3 . Here rate is an integer variable,
not a time derivative. No real-valued trajectory, diﬀerential inequality, vorticity maximum
principle, or connection of this predicate to Navier–Stokes is proved. An analytical max-rate
law and its PDE bridge remain separate missing inputs; this rearrangement does not supply
them. Finer decompositions of the open BKM input belong to the continuation repository
and are not formalized here.
    Open. PeriodicBKMIntegrability; every item of the ledger above marked open; and
therefore Statement (B) itself. The Clay Millennium Problem is not solved by this work.
