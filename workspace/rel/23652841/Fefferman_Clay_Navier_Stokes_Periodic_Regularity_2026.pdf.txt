Abstract
We present a self-contained Lean 4 formalization (leanprover/lean4:v4.30.0-rc1,
Mathlib 4, 184 kernel-veriﬁed theorems across 5 modules with zero sorry and zero nonstandard axioms outside [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]) of Charles L. Fefferman’s oﬃcial periodic 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes problem statement (Existence
and Smoothness of the Navier–Stokes Equation, Clay Mathematics Institute, 2006, Statement (B) on R3 /Z3 ) together with unconditional geometric/kinematic identities and explicit
conditional reductions to Beale–Kato–Majda continuation.
Scope and Honesty Statement: This work does not solve Clay Problem (B) or (A).
Speciﬁcally:
1. We prove unconditionally that spatial Z3 -periodicity excludes any non-zero global linear background strain ustrain (x) = Ax on R3 (z3_periodic_linear_eq_zero), including the global uniaxial Burgers background strain A = diag(−α/2, −α/2, α) with
α ̸= 0 (burgersLinearStrain_not_z3_periodic). Crucially, torus periodicity rules
out only a global linear background strain: localized Burgers-like stretched vortex
structures can still form locally on T3 = R3 /Z3 , so global linear strain exclusion does
not yield unconditional Clay (B).
2. We prove unconditionally the pointwise Biot–Savart direction-alignment depletion
identity (biot_savart_det_aligned_eq_zero, aligned_vorticity_stretching_integrand_zero,
biot_savart_det_difference_identity), the parabolic scale-invariance identities of
3D Navier–Stokes under uλ (x, t) = λu(λx, λ2 t) (navier_stokes_vorticity_dt_scaling_exponent,
navier_stokes_scaling_homogeneity_deg6, unnormalized_quartic_scaling_mismatch),
and the cubic comparison ODE ceiling M (t) ≤ max(M (0), CCZ /κ′′ ) (comparison_ode_ceiling)
whenever a diﬀerentiable trajectory satisﬁes M ′ (t) ≤ CCZ M (t)2 − κ′′ M (t)3 .

3. We formulate the analytic closure hypotheses PeriodicDirectionDepletionBound ν,
PeriodicTetherClosingBound ν, and PeriodicBKMContinuation ν as explicit Propvalued hypotheses (never as axioms) and prove the conditional reductions clay_B_exact_of_periodic_tet
and clay_B_exact_of_torus_direction_depletion. Whether arbitrary smooth periodic Navier–Stokes ﬂows on T3 satisfy PeriodicDirectionDepletionBound ν or
PeriodicTetherClosingBound ν remains an open problem.
All Lean 4 proofs are audited in-kernel by AxiomAudit.lean and attested with an Ed25519signed titan-audit certiﬁcate and sealguard Merkle-root provenance manifest.

Introduction and Explicit Proved vs. Open Status

In his oﬃcial Clay Mathematics Institute problem description [1], Charles L. Feﬀerman formulates the periodic existence and smoothness problem (Statement (B)) for the three-dimensional
incompressible Navier–Stokes equations on T3 = R3 /Z3 . Formalizing PDE regularity statements
in interactive theorem provers requires extreme care to avoid speciﬁcation gaming, vacuous hypotheses, or conﬂating conditional reductions with unconditional proofs.
In this paper and its companion Lean 4 repository (BenFrohman/fefferman-clay-navier-stokes),
we maintain a strict, machine-checked separation between:
• Unconditionally proved theorems in Lean 4 (veriﬁed from Mathlib’s standard axioms [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound] with zero sorry and zero custom
axiom declarations), and

• Open analytic hypotheses (PeriodicDirectionDepletionBound ν, PeriodicTetherClosingBound ν
and the classical PDE continuation package PeriodicBKMContinuation ν), which are deﬁned as Props and passed explicitly as hypotheses to our conditional reduction theorems.
Table 1 provides a complete, transparent accounting of what is unconditionally proved and
what remains open.

Remark 3.2 (Crucial Scope Warning: Why Global Linear Strain Exclusion Does Not Solve
Clay (B)). Theorem burgersLinearStrain_not_z3_periodic rules out only a global linear
background strain u(x) = Ax ∫across R3 /Z3 . On the compact torus T3 , a smooth periodic
