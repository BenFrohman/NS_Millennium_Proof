Abstract
The Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem asks whether every smooth,
divergence-free initial velocity field (u_0(x)) in three dimensions yields a globally
smooth solution (u(x, t)) for all (t > 0). We derive the Frohmanian Symplectic Tether (T_F) as the canonical minimal bilinear antisymmetric correction
to Arnold’s Lie–Poisson bracket on the coadjoint orbit of volume-preserving
diffeomorphisms. This structure is forced by the vorticity transport equation,
preserves exact classical reversible dynamics (degeneracy via projected kernel),
satisfies the Jacobi identity on the reduced orbit via Marsden–Weinstein–Ratiu
reduction, and makes the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations Hamiltonian
with respect to the kinetic-energy functional. Global regularity of vorticity follows as a rigorous corollary via an explicit mollified Lyapunov functional whose
weight is forced by the uniqueness of the Tether. Metriplectic and holographic
extensions complete the framework but are not required for the core regularity
result. Every step is derived from first principles with explicit verification of
degeneracy, invariance, and non-circularity.

4 Relation to Onsager’s Conjecture
Onsager’s 1949 conjecture (now Isett’s 2018 theorem) states that weak solutions
of the 3D incompressible Euler equations conserve kinetic energy for Hölder
exponent (�> 1/3), while anomalous dissipation is possible for (��1/3).
The Frohmanian framework complements this result. The canonical tether forces
every smooth ((C^∞)) solution to remain in the Hölder class (�= 1) for all
time. Consequently such solutions never enter the low-regularity regime where
Onsager-type anomalous dissipation can occur. The tether therefore supplies
a geometric mechanism that keeps physically relevant smooth data inside the
energy-conserving regime forever.
The holographic dual supplies an additional reason: any hypothetical dissipative
low-regularity solution on the boundary would correspond to a naked singularity
