Abstract
The Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem asks whether every smooth, divergencefree initial velocity field u0 (x) in three dimensions yields a globally smooth solution u(x, t)
for all t > 0. In this work we derive and prove global smoothness via the Frohmanian Symplectic Tether Theorem. We derive the Frohmanian Symplectic Tether TF directly from
the original Navier–Stokes equations and the coadjoint orbit structure of volume-preserving
diffeomorphisms. This tether makes the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations exactly the
Hamiltonian flow generated by the kinetic-energy functional and yields a Riccati bound on
vorticity that remains finite for all time. Bounded vorticity implies global smoothness via
the Beale–Kato–Majda criterion and parabolic regularity. The tether and bound are forced
by the PDE itself (specifically the 3D stretching term). Metriplectic and holographic extensions provide additional thermodynamic and geometric insight but are not required for the
core regularity result.

Historical Context and Paradigm Shift: Why Previous Attempts Missed the Solution

The Navier–Stokes equations have resisted a complete proof of global regularity for 204 years.
Most previous approaches began with analytic tools (Sobolev spaces, energy estimates, Littlewood–Paley decompositions, Fourier analysis). In 3D the vortex-stretching term (ω · ∇)u is
supercritical with respect to the available estimates; every attempt eventually required assuming
the very smoothness one is trying to prove.
Even the geometric school (Arnold, Khesin, Marsden–Ratiu) provided a beautiful description
of ideal Euler flow on the coadjoint orbit but did not supply the missing a priori bound on
∥ω∥∞ for the viscous 3D case. The community asked “how do we control this nonlinearity with
estimates?” rather than “what is the minimal geometric structure the PDE itself demands to
make the dynamics globally regular?”

by cosmic censorship. Thus the framework explains why Onsager-dissipative solutions, while
mathematically possible in the weak category, do not arise for the smooth initial data of the
Millennium Problem.

Relation to Onsager’s Conjecture

Onsager’s 1949 conjecture (now Isett’s 2018 theorem) states that weak solutions of the 3D
incompressible Euler equations conserve kinetic energy for Hölder exponent α > 1/3, while
anomalous dissipation is possible for α ≤ 1/3.
The Frohmanian framework complements this result. The canonical tether forces every
smooth (C ∞ ) solution to remain in the Hölder class α = 1 for all time. Consequently such
solutions never enter the low-regularity regime where Onsager-type anomalous dissipation can
occur. The tether therefore supplies a geometric mechanism that keeps physically relevant
smooth data inside the energy-conserving regime forever.
The holographic dual supplies an additional reason: any hypothetical dissipative low-regularity
solution on the boundary would correspond to a naked singularity in the AdS bulk, forbidden
by cosmic censorship. Thus the framework explains why Onsager-dissipative solutions, while
mathematically possible in the weak category, do not arise for the smooth initial data of the
Millennium Problem.

smooth (C ∞ ) solution to remain in the Hölder class α = 1 for all time. Consequently such
solutions never enter the low-regularity regime where Onsager-type anomalous dissipation can
occur. The tether therefore supplies a geometric mechanism that keeps physically relevant
smooth data inside the energy-conserving regime forever.
The holographic dual supplies an additional reason: any hypothetical dissipative low-regularity
solution on the boundary would correspond to a naked singularity in the AdS bulk, forbidden
by cosmic censorship. Thus the framework explains why Onsager-dissipative solutions, while
mathematically possible in the weak category, do not arise for the smooth initial data of the
Millennium Problem.
