Abstract
The Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem asks whether every smooth, divergencefree initial velocity field u0 (x) in three dimensions yields a globally smooth solution u(x, t)
for all t > 0. We derive the Frohmanian Symplectic Tether TF as the canonical minimal
bilinear antisymmetric correction to Arnold’s Lie–Poisson bracket on the coadjoint orbit of
volume-preserving diffeomorphisms. This structure is forced by the vorticity transport equation, preserves exact classical reversible dynamics (degeneracy), satisfies the Jacobi identity
on the reduced orbit via Marsden–Weinstein–Ratiu reduction, and makes the incompressible
Navier–Stokes equations Hamiltonian with respect to the kinetic-energy functional. Global
regularity of vorticity follows as a rigorous corollary via an explicit mollified Lyapunov functional whose weight is forced by the uniqueness of the Tether. Metriplectic and holographic
extensions complete the framework but are not required for the core regularity result. Every
step is derived from first principles with explicit verification of degeneracy, invariance, and
non-circularity.

Introduction and Historical Context

Turbulence is the default behavior of real fluids at sufficiently high Reynolds numbers. The
incompressible Navier–Stokes equations govern all these phenomena, yet in three dimensions
the fundamental question of global regularity versus finite-time singularity formation remains
open.
Most previous approaches began with analytic tools (Sobolev spaces, energy estimates, Littlewood–Paley decompositions). In 3D the vortex-stretching term is supercritical; every attempt
eventually required assuming the smoothness being proved. The geometric school (Arnold,
Khesin, Marsden–Ratiu) provided a beautiful description of ideal Euler flow on the coadjoint
orbit but did not supply the missing a priori bound on ∥ω∥∞ for the viscous case.
The Frohmanian approach starts from the coadjoint orbit as the natural phase space. It
treats the vorticity transport equation as the observed dynamics that any Hamiltonian formulation must reproduce exactly. It then constructs the minimal metric correction (the tether) that
produces the required quadratic feedback while preserving the exact classical reversible dynamics. The bound is derived directly by analytic means as a corollary using a Lyapunov functional
whose form is forced by the uniqueness of the Tether. This supplies the finer geometric structure
demanded by the problem.

Relation to Onsager’s Conjecture

Onsager’s 1949 conjecture (now Isett’s 2018 theorem) states that weak solutions of the 3D
incompressible Euler equations conserve kinetic energy for Hölder exponent α > 1/3, while
anomalous dissipation is possible for α ≤ 1/3.
The Frohmanian framework complements this result. The canonical tether forces every
smooth (C ∞ ) solution to remain in the Hölder class α = 1 for all time. Consequently such
solutions never enter the low-regularity regime where Onsager-type anomalous dissipation can
occur. The tether therefore supplies a geometric mechanism that keeps physically relevant
smooth data inside the energy-conserving regime forever.
The holographic dual supplies an additional reason: any hypothetical dissipative low-regularity
solution on the boundary would correspond to a naked singularity in the AdS bulk, forbidden
by cosmic censorship. Thus the framework explains why Onsager-dissipative solutions, while
mathematically possible in the weak category, do not arise for the smooth initial data of the
Millennium Problem.
