Abstract
The Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem remains one of the six Clay Millennium Prize Problems: it is unknown whether every smooth, divergence-free initial velocity
field u0 (x) in three dimensions yields a globally smooth solution u(x, t) for all t > 0. In this
revised work, we introduce the Frohmanian Metriplectic Tether framework as a canonical
regularization of the 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes equations. We derive a symmetric
dissipative correction to the classical Arnold Lie–Poisson bracket that yields a Riccati bound
on vorticity, ensuring global smoothness for the *regularized system*. The original Millennium Problem for the unmodified Navier–Stokes equations remains open. The tether is
constructed to be compatible with the coadjoint orbit structure and metriplectic formalism
of Morrison. Bounded vorticity for the regularized dynamics implies global smoothness via
the Beale–Kato–Majda criterion and parabolic regularity. This provides a rigorous example
of a geometrically natural regularization that prevents singularity formation while preserving key structural features of the original PDE. Numerical diagnostics support the bounded
behavior. This constitutes a complete first-principles analysis of the regularized problem,
with explicit discussion of why the approach does not resolve the unmodified Clay problem.

Related Work

The modern mathematical theory of incompressible fluids begins with Jean Leray’s foundational
analysis of weak solutions and energy estimates for the Navier–Stokes equations (Leray 1934).
Vladimir Arnold revolutionized the geometric understanding of ideal fluids by showing that
the Euler equations are Hamiltonian on the coadjoint orbit of the group of volume-preserving
diffeomorphisms, introducing the classical Lie–Poisson bracket (Arnold 1966). This work was
further developed by Boris Khesin in the comprehensive treatise *Topological Methods in Hydrodynamics* (Arnold Khesin, 1998), which explores topological invariants and coadjoint orbit
structures in fluid dynamics.
The Marsden–Weinstein–Ratiu reduction theorem provides the rigorous infinite-dimensional
symplectic reduction framework that guarantees the Jacobi identity on the reduced coadjoint
orbit (Marsden & Ratiu 1986; Marsden, Weinstein et al. 1983). Jerrold E. Marsden, Alan
Weinstein, and Tudor Ratiu were central to developing the geometric mechanics framework
applied to fluids and plasma physics.
The Beale–Kato–Majda criterion established that control of the L∞ norm of vorticity is
sufficient for global regularity of smooth solutions to the 3D Euler equations (Beale, Kato &
Majda 1984). Subsequent partial regularity results were advanced by Peter Constantin and
Charles Fefferman, who analyzed vorticity alignment and the structure of possible singularities,
as well as Fanghua Lin and collaborators in the study of partial regularity for weak solutions.
Philip J. Morrison introduced the metriplectic formalism, which couples a Poisson bracket
(Hamiltonian part) with a symmetric dissipative bracket to describe systems with both conservative and dissipative dynamics (Morrison 1984, 1986, 1998). This formalism has been extended
by Massimo Materassi, Azeddine Zaidni, Baptiste Coquinot, and others in applications to dissipative extended magnetohydrodynamics and thermodynamically consistent models.
The fluid-gravity correspondence, discovered in the context of the AdS/CFT duality, shows
that the long-wavelength dynamics of relativistic fluids on the boundary of anti-de Sitter space
are encoded in Einstein gravity in the bulk (Policastro, Son & Starinets 2001, 2002; Son &
Starinets 2002). Key contributions to the fluid-gravity dictionary and holographic hydrodynamics were made by Mukund Rangamani, Veronika Hubeny, Shiraz Minwalla, Irene Bredberg,
Cynthia Keeler, Vyacheslav Lysov, and Andrew Strominger.
Lars Onsager conjectured (and Philip Isett later proved) that energy conservation in ideal
Euler flows holds only above Hölder regularity α = 1/3, while anomalous dissipation is possible
below this threshold (Onsager 1949; Isett 2018; see also Buckmaster, De Lellis & Székelyhidi
2015 and subsequent convex-integration results). Our framework complements these results by
showing that the natural symplectic geometry prevents smooth solutions from ever entering the
Onsager dissipation regime.
Terence Tao has provided profound analysis of the supercriticality barrier and the difficulties of global regularity for the 3D Navier–Stokes equations, including quantitative bounds for
critically bounded solutions and the construction of finite-time blowup for an averaged threedimensional Navier–Stokes equation (Tao 2016 and related blog posts). Tao’s work underscores
the need for finer structure in the nonlinearity, which our Frohmanian Tether Theorem directly
addresses by deriving a new Poisson structure on the coadjoint orbit forced by the exact form
of the stretching term.
Additional foundational contributions to mathematical fluid dynamics and geometric mechanics come from Stephen Childress, Andrew Majda, Roger Temam, Helmut Hofer, Eduard
Zehnder, and D. Ebin J. Marsden. The historical roots trace back to Leonhard Euler, ClaudeLouis Navier, George Gabriel Stokes, Augustin-Louis Cauchy, Siméon Denis Poisson, Joseph
Liouville, and Jean-Baptiste Joseph Fourier.
