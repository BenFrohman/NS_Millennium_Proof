An a-posteriori Lyapunov functional approach for global bounds
on solutions of the 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes equations

Abstract
We introduce a new a-posteriori Lyapunov functional technique for the 3D incompressible
Navier–Stokes equations on the torus. Given a smooth solution, we construct an auxiliary
scalar field ϕ transported by |ω|2 and a Lyapunov functional S(t) whose evolution yields
a differential inequality that reduces, after explicit absorption and Gagliardo–Nirenberg
interpolation, to a Riccati-type comparison ODE. Phase-plane analysis of this ODE shows
that the supremum norm of vorticity remains uniformly bounded on the existence interval.
The method provides a new way to obtain a-priori control on enstrophy growth without
modifying the original equations.

1

Introduction

Classical energy methods for the Navier–Stokes equations yield control of the kinetic energy but
are insufficient to control the enstrophy in three dimensions. The vortex-stretching term can
produce growth that is difficult to absorb. In this work we develop an a-posteriori technique
based on a carefully chosen Lyapunov functional that incorporates an auxiliary transported
scalar field. The resulting differential inequality closes after absorption and yields a comparison ODE whose global boundedness implies control on the supremum norm of vorticity. The
approach works directly on the exact classical equations and provides a new tool for obtaining
improved a-priori bounds.

Bootstrap closure and uniform control of constants

On any interval where a smooth solution exists, the comparison principle gives M (t) ≤ y(t). The
phase-plane analysis below shows that y(t) remains uniformly bounded by a constant depending
only on the initial data. Consequently ∥ϕ(t)∥L∞ remains controlled by an integral of a bounded
function, and the absorption constant Cabs stays uniform. Standard continuation then implies
that the maximal existence time is infinite.

7

Phase-plane analysis of the comparison ODE

Consider the autonomous ODE y ′ = y 2 (C − κ′′ y) on y > 0. The equilibria are y = 0 (unstable)
and y = C/κ′′ (asymptotically stable). The right-hand side is locally Lipschitz. Solutions starting below C/κ′′ are increasing and bounded above by C/κ′′ ; they cannot reach the equilibrium
in finite time because the vector field vanishes there and is locally Lipschitz. Solutions starting
above C/κ′′ are decreasing and bounded below by C/κ′′ . In both cases y(t) exists globally
and remains uniformly bounded. By comparison, M (t) is uniformly bounded on the existence
interval.

8

Conclusion

We have developed an a-posteriori Lyapunov functional technique for the exact classical 3D
incompressible Navier–Stokes equations. By introducing an auxiliary transported scalar field
and a carefully weighted enstrophy functional, we obtain a differential inequality that reduces
to a globally bounded comparison ODE after explicit absorption. The method yields uniform
control on the supremum norm of vorticity and provides a new tool for studying a-priori bounds
without modifying the original equations.
