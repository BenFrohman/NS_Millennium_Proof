     A LEAN-CERTIFIED CONDITIONAL IMPLICATION FOR THE 3D
  INCOMPRESSIBLE NAVIER–STOKES EQUATIONS: HYPOTHESIS B AND
  THE BEALE–KATO–MAJDA CRITERION IMPLY GLOBAL REGULARITY
               OF MAXIMAL CLASSICAL SOLUTIONS

                                       BENJAMIN STANLEY FROHMAN


        Abstract. This paper states only what a Lean 4 kernel certificate proves in the companion
        repository. Let ν > 0 and let u0 be a smooth, periodic, divergence-free initial velocity on R3
        (the period cell models T3 ). In the Lean development, a maximal classical solution of periodic
        incompressible Navier–Stokes is an object of type NSSolution. Lean proves: if Hypothesis B
        holds for some bound C⋆ , and if the named Beale–Kato–Majda hypothesis    ∫T     BKMCriterion holds,
        then every such maximal classical solution is global (T ⋆ = ⊤) and 0 ∥∇u(t)∥L∞ dt < ∞ for
        every finite T > 0. Those two hypotheses are recorded below exactly as they are written in
        Lean. They are assumptions, not theorems of this development. Clay Millennium Problem State-
        ment (B) remains open [2]: the unconditioned statement is recorded in Lean only as the proposition
        analytic_bridge_unconditional_Statement, which has no proof. Kato local existence, Fujita–
        Kato small-data global regularity, a strain-only continuation criterion, logarithmic Sobolev bounds,
        and a parabolic bootstrap are not formalized here and are not claimed.

                                           1. What is certified
   The companion repository https://github.com/BenFrohman/frohmanian-tether-conditional-regularity
contains a Lean 4 formalization. The only regularity theorem claimed in this paper is the one Lean
proves under two named hypotheses. The kernel certificate is the oﬀicial GitHub Actions artifact
for the commit and run recorded in the certificate block below. That artifact includes the repos-
itory’s own lake-manifest.json, its SHA-256, the toolchain, elan/lake/lean versions, the full
teed lake build log, and #print axioms output. A hand-written summary is not a certificate.
   An independent review of whether the Lean encodings of BKMCriterion and HypothesisB are
faithful to the textbook Beale–Kato–Majda criterion, and whether they are vacuous, is in progress.
This paper transcribes the Lean hypotheses as they stand. If that review requires a rewrite of either
hypothesis, the paper and the Truth Gate lock will be updated to match, and nothing built on a
rewritten hypothesis will be treated as already passed.

                    2. The Lean hypotheses and the certified implication
  In Lean, E3 is R3 with the Euclidean structure, and periodicity is periodicity of period 1 in each
coordinate. A structure IsClassicalNS records that (u, p) is a classical solution of periodic incom-
pressible Navier–Stokes with viscosity ν > 0 on the lifespan [0, T ⋆ ): the initial condition, periodicity,
smoothness, divergence-free constraint, and pointwise momentum equation. An NSSolution pack-
ages a positive viscosity, a lifespan, fields u and p, a proof of IsClassicalNS, and maximality.

uation criterion [1]. It is not proved in Lean.
Definition 2.3 (Analytic bridge statement, Lean AnalyticBridgeStatement). The analytic bridge
statement says: for every ν > 0 and every smooth, periodic, divergence-free initial datum u0 , every
maximal classical solution satisfies T ⋆ = ⊤ and gradIntegral(sol, T ) < ⊤ for every T > 0.
Theorem 2.4 (Conditional implication, Lean analyticBridge_of_formB / analyticBridge_of_formB_forall).
For every bound C⋆ : R → R, if Hypothesis B holds for C⋆ and if BKMCriterion holds, then the
analytic bridge statement holds.

The content of that bound for a zero dummy norm is trivial (kp_trivial); its intended content is

                                      3. Open formalization items
   The following claims appeared as theorems in earlier versions of this manuscript. They are not
formalized in the companion Lean development, and this paper does not present them as proved
or as kernel-certified:
      • Kato local existence and a deterministic lifespan bound for arbitrary data in H s (s > 5/2);
      • Fujita–Kato global regularity for suﬀiciently small data in Ḣ 1/2 ;
      • a strain-only continuation criterion, logarithmic Sobolev bounds relating ∥∇u∥L∞ to ∥ω∥L∞ ,
        a double-exponential H s majorant, and a parabolic bootstrap to C ∞ ;
      • the geometric metriplectic completion of Arnold’s Lie–Poisson framework on SDiﬀ(T3 ) as
        a derivation of the Navier–Stokes equations on the torus (the repository does contain ab-
        stract degeneracy and symmetry lemmas in FrohmanianTripleArchitecture.lean; those
        lemmas are not a kernel-checked derivation of the PDE on T3 ).
Formalizing any of these in Lean, with only the standard axioms and no sorry, is left open.
