\begin{abstract}
For a smooth divergence-free solution of the unforced periodic Navier--Stokes equations, the vorticity maximum $M(t)=\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}$ satisfies the cubic source bound $M'\le C_{\mathrm{CZ}} M^2$ at a spatial maximum. The comparison equation $y'=C y^2$ can become infinite in finite time, so this bound does not close the Beale--Kato--Majda integral. A quadratic reduction of the strain would replace the pairing $C_{\mathrm{CZ}} M^3$ by $C_{\mathrm{CZ}} M^3-\kappa M^4$ and the rate by $C_{\mathrm{CZ}} M^2-\kappa M^3$. That reduction is recorded here as an open identity. It is not derived from the unmodified vorticity equation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}
Let $u$ be a smooth divergence-free solution of the unforced periodic Navier--Stokes equations on $\mathbb{T}^3$, with viscosity $\nu\ge 0$, and let $\omega=\nabla\times u$. Write $M(t)=\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}$. The vorticity equation is
\[
\partial_t\omega+(u\cdot\nabla)\omega=(\omega\cdot\nabla)u+\nu\Delta\omega.
\]
Fix a time at which $|\omega|$ attains a spatial maximum at $x$, and write $M=|\omega(x)|$.

\section{The proved pairing}
At a critical point of $|\omega|^2$, the transport pairing vanishes:
\[
\omega\cdot(u\cdot\nabla)\omega=\tfrac12 u\cdot\nabla(|\omega|^2)=0.
\]
The identity $\omega\cdot\Delta\omega=\tfrac12\Delta(|\omega|^2)-|\nabla\omega|^2$ and $\Delta(|\omega|^2)\le 0$ at a maximum give $\omega\cdot\Delta\omega\le 0$. Biot--Savart recovers $\nabla u$ from $\omega$ by a Calder\'on--Zygmund operator, which is bounded from $L^\infty$ into $\mathrm{BMO}$ and not on $L^\infty$. The usable pointwise bound is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:source}
\omega(x)\cdot\bigl((\omega(x)\cdot\nabla)u(x)\bigr)\le C_{\mathrm{CZ}} M^3.
\end{equation}
Thus $\omega(x)\cdot\partial_t\omega(x)\le C_{\mathrm{CZ}} M^3$. Along the maximizing point, $\omega\cdot\partial_t\omega=M M'$ for $M>0$, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rate-source}
M'\le C_{\mathrm{CZ}} M^2.
\end{equation}
The comparison equation $y'=C y^2$ has the solution $y(t)=y_0/(1-C y_0 t)$ for $C y_0 t<1$, which becomes infinite at $t=1/(C y_0)$. The Beale--Kato--Majda continuation criterion requires $\int\|\omega\|_{L^\infty}\,dt<\infty$. The bound \eqref{eq:rate-source} does not make that integral finite.

\section{The missing identity}
The open line is a quadratic reduction of the same pairing. It asserts that there exists $\kappa>0$, depending only on the initial datum and on $\nu$, and not on $M(t)$, such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:missing}
\omega(x)\cdot\bigl((\omega(x)\cdot\nabla)u(x)\bigr)
\le\bigl(C_{\mathrm{CZ}} M-\kappa M^2\bigr)M^2.
\end{equation}
The right-hand side expands by algebra:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:expand}
\bigl(C_{\mathrm{CZ}} M-\kappa M^2\bigr)M^2=C_{\mathrm{CZ}} M^3-\kappa M^4.
\end{equation}
The subtracted term is $\kappa M^4$. It is not $\kappa M^5$. If \eqref{eq:missing} held, transport and viscosity would give
\[
\omega(x)\cdot\partial_t\omega(x)\le C_{\mathrm{CZ}} M^3-\kappa M^4,
\]
and, for $M>0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sink}
M'\le C_{\mathrm{CZ}} M^2-\kappa M^3=M^2\bigl(C_{\mathrm{CZ}}-\kappa M\bigr).
\end{equation}
For $y>C_{\mathrm{CZ}}/\kappa$ the field $C y^2-\kappa y^3$ is negative, so a trajectory obeying \eqref{eq:sink} cannot cross the ceiling $C_{\mathrm{CZ}}/\kappa$. A bounded continuous maximum has finite integral on every finite time interval, and Beale--Kato--Majda would continue the solution. Equation \eqref{eq:missing} is not an identity of the unmodified vorticity equation.

\section{What the hypothesis is}
Write $\mathrm{rate}$ for an upper bound on $M'$. The rate form of \eqref{eq:sink} is the assumption
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hypothesis}
\mathrm{rate}\le M\bigl(C_{\mathrm{CZ}} M-\kappa M^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
Expanding \eqref{eq:hypothesis} is ring arithmetic and returns $C_{\mathrm{CZ}} M^2-\kappa M^3$. The assumption already contains the reduced factor. It does not derive \eqref{eq:missing}.

The weight $1+\kappa M^2\ge 0$ multiplies the proved source and produces another source,
\[
C_{\mathrm{CZ}} M^3\bigl(1+\kappa M^2\bigr)=C_{\mathrm{CZ}} M^3+C_{\mathrm{CZ}}\kappa M^5.
\]
The term of degree five is not the sink. A degeneracy $B(F,H)\equiv 0$ on a kinetic-energy Hamiltonian leaves the energy field classical and does not insert $-\kappa M^2$ into the strain. The logarithmic repair $\|\nabla u\|_{L^\infty}\lesssim\|\omega\|_{L^\infty}(1+\log^+\|\omega\|_{H^s})$ assumes control of a higher norm and is an integral criterion, not a derivation of \eqref{eq:missing}.

\section{Record}
The checked statement is \eqref{eq:rate-source}. The open statement is the reduction of the pairing from $C_{\mathrm{CZ}} M^3$ to $C_{\mathrm{CZ}} M^3-\kappa M^4$. No theorem in this note closes that reduction. A formal certificate that expands \eqref{eq:hypothesis} is an expansion of a hypothesis. It is not a proof of global regularity for the periodic Navier--Stokes equations.
