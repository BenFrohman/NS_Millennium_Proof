\begin{abstract}
This addendum separates the uniform-envelope assumption in the checked Lean
reduction from a genuinely differential assumption on the actual vorticity
supremum. It records the conditional comparison proved in the accompanying source
patch, tests a proposed coercivity mechanism on explicit periodic solutions, and
identifies the remaining analytic obligation. It is not a proof of general
Navier--Stokes regularity. Earlier published PDFs and archives are unchanged.
\end{abstract}

\section{The corrected distinction}
The baseline source is \lean{BenFrohman/NS_Millennium_Proof} at commit
\lean{c98fe859867affeba4508b3e51583cfa6b20087c}.
Historically \lean{DirectionCurvatureBound} abbreviates
\lean{PeriodicDirectionDepletionBound}. Its actual body assumes that, for every
smooth divergence-free periodic datum, there are positive constants $C,\kappa$
such that every represented local solution satisfies
\[
 M(t)\leq\max\{M(0),C/\kappa\},\qquad 0\leq t<T.
\]
Here $M(t)=\|\operatorname{curl}u(t)\|_\infty$. The constants may depend on
viscosity and the datum, but not on the solution or its lifespan. This definition
does not assert a differential inequality. The accurate public name is now
\lean{UniformVorticityEnvelopeBound}; the original body, historical aliases and
original conditional theorem are preserved.

The separate \lean{CubicVorticityDifferentialBound}$(u,\tau,C,\kappa)$ explicitly
requires $\tau\geq0$, $C,\kappa>0$, bounded spatial vorticity-norm ranges, ordinary
differentiability of the actual $M$ at every point of $[0,\tau]$, and
\[
 M'(t)\leq C M(t)^2-\kappa M(t)^3\quad(0\leq t\leq\tau).
\]
The boundedness guard matters for the real-valued conditional supremum used in
Lean. No differential estimate or differentiability of a supremum is inferred
merely from smoothness of the velocity.

\section{The checked conditional consequences}
The existing \lean{comparison_ode_ceiling}, with all its hypotheses, gives the
compact envelope above. The new source proves this for the actual curl supremum,
then bounds the actual curl pointwise using spatial boundedness. On an actual
\lean{IsPeriodicSmoothSolutionOn}, compatible compact hypotheses for every
$0\leq\tau<T$ with the \emph{same} coefficients give the envelope on $[0,T)$;
the initial identity comes from that solution's definition. A zero-trajectory
regression shows that the differential predicate is inhabited.

\lean{clay_reduces_to_uniform_vorticity_envelope} accurately names the existing
conditional reduction: the uniform envelope for every positive viscosity,
\emph{and} the separately assumed periodic BKM continuation principle, imply
\lean{Clay_B_exact}. Neither premise is established by that reduction.

The Stage6 additions check the derivative of $M_0e^{-at}$, its cubic estimate
with $C=\kappa M_0$, and the fixed-coefficient obstruction below. These are
\emph{scalar} calculus and algebra theorems. Assembly of the following vector
fields into the Lean solution structure and identification of their suprema are
not newly formalized here; the explicit PDE calculations are separate
mathematical checks.

\section{What the PDE identity supplies}
For a classical unforced incompressible solution, let $\omega=\operatorname{curl}u$,
$S=(\nabla u+(\nabla u)^T)/2$ and $\rho=|\omega|$. Then
\[
 (\partial_t+u\cdot\nabla)\frac{|\omega|^2}{2}
 =\omega\cdot S\omega+\nu\Delta\frac{|\omega|^2}{2}-\nu|\nabla\omega|^2.
\]
Where $\rho>0$, write $\omega=\rho\xi$, $|\xi|=1$. Differentiating gives
\begin{align*}
 |\nabla\omega|^2&=|\nabla\rho|^2+\rho^2|\nabla\xi|^2,\\
 (\partial_t+u\cdot\nabla-\nu\Delta)\rho
 &=\rho\,\xi\cdot S\xi-\nu\rho|\nabla\xi|^2.
\end{align*}
At a positive spatial maximum $\nabla\rho=0$ and $\Delta\rho\leq0$. These facts do
not give a lower bound on direction curvature. A general maximum-in-time argument
also needs a justified one-sided/Dini derivative treatment of maximizing-branch
switches, not an automatic ordinary derivative of $M$.

\subsection{Beltrami family: fixed coefficients versus data dependence}
On the unit torus take $\nu>0$, $t\geq0$, $k=2\pi$, $a=\nu k^2$, $A>0$, and
\[
 u_A(t,x)=Ae^{-at}(\sin(kx_3),\cos(kx_3),0),\qquad p=0.
\]
Direct differentiation gives $\operatorname{div}u_A=0$, $(u_A\cdot\nabla)u_A=0$,
$\Delta u_A=-k^2u_A$, $\partial_tu_A=\nu\Delta u_A$ and
$\operatorname{curl}u_A=ku_A$. Consequently
\[
 M_A(t)=kAe^{-at},\qquad M_A'=-aM_A.
\]
At zero, the cubic estimate is equivalent to
$\kappa M_A(0)^2-CM_A(0)-a\leq0$. For fixed $C\geq0$, $\kappa>0$ and $a\geq0$,
this fails at sufficiently large amplitude. The Lean theorem
\lean{fixed_cubic_constants_fail_for_some_amplitude} proves the quantified scalar
obstruction. This does \emph{not} refute the data-dependent envelope: choosing
$C=\kappa M_A(0)$ makes the cubic right-hand side
$\kappa M_A(t)^2(M_A(0)-M_A(t))\geq0$, while $M_A'\leq0$. The latter special-case
sufficient estimate is also checked in Lean for the scalar profile.

\subsection{Single shear: direction-only coercivity fails}
Instead take the actual periodic heat shear
\[
 v_A(t,x)=Ae^{-at}(\sin(kx_3),0,0),\qquad p=0.
\]
The same divergence, convection and heat calculation verifies the unforced
Navier--Stokes equations. Here
\[
 \omega_A(t,x)=(0,kAe^{-at}\cos(kx_3),0).
\]
At $x_3=0$, its norm attains the positive global maximum $M=kAe^{-at}$.
In a neighborhood $\cos(kx_3)>0$, so $\xi=(0,1,0)$ is constant and
$|\nabla\xi|^2=0$. Thus the bare premise
\[
 \kappa M^2\leq\nu|\nabla\xi|^2
\]
from \lean{viscous_direction_dissipation_bridge} fails for \emph{every}
$\kappa>0$, even if it depends on this datum. This refutes promotion of that
premise to an unconditional assertion at every positive maximum, not the
existing implication which assumes it. Nor does it refute a different estimate
restricted above a data-dependent threshold. The actual damping here comes from
amplitude curvature:
\[
 \omega_A\cdot\Delta\omega_A=-k^2M^2,\qquad M'=-\nu k^2M.
\]
Discarding amplitude curvature loses this real viscous contribution.

\subsection{Scaling is not a coercivity proof}
For the parabolic rescaling $u_\lambda(t,x)=\lambda u(\lambda^2t,\lambda x)$,
one has $M_\lambda(t)=\lambda^2M(\lambda^2t)$ and the time derivative scales
by $\lambda^4$ wherever it exists. A cubic inequality therefore transforms with
$C_\lambda=C$ and $\kappa_\lambda=\kappa/\lambda^2$, not fixed positive
$\kappa$. Likewise $\nu|\nabla\xi|^2$ scales by $\lambda^2$ whereas $M^2$ scales
by $\lambda^4$. This does not contradict data-dependent coefficients. On the
fixed unit torus the displayed transformation preserves unit periodicity for
positive integer $\lambda$; arbitrary domain rescalings are not the same torus.
The separate scale-homogeneous premise $\kappa_{\rm eff}M\leq\nu|\nabla\xi|^2$
in the source only yields $M'\leq(C-\kappa_{\rm eff})M^2$, not cubic damping
in $M$. Scaling supplies neither coercivity premise.

\section{Endpoint representation and remaining obligation}
The Lean local-solution structure constrains only nonnegative times. Extend the
zero forward solution by $(\sin(kx_3),\cos(kx_3),0)$ for negative times. It is
still the zero solution on $[0,T)$, but its actual vorticity supremum jumps from
$k$ to zero at time zero. Hence ordinary two-sided differentiability of $M$ at
zero cannot follow for every represented solution. This is not an obstruction to
right-sided PDE smoothness. The globally zero trajectory, unlike this other
extension, passes the new regression. The compact differential predicate is an
explicit additional condition on a given trajectory, not a universal PDE theorem.

A sufficient net-source estimate at the relevant positive maxima would be
\[
 \omega\cdot S\omega+\nu\omega\cdot\Delta\omega
 \leq CM^3-\kappa M^4,
\]
with correctly quantified positive coefficients uniform in time and lifespan.
Division by $M$ then gives the cubic rate after a justified maximum-derivative
argument. The repository proves the algebra conditionally, not this general
source estimate. An endpoint $L^\infty$ Calderon--Zygmund bound cannot silently
supply it. A suitable source estimate and a one-sided/Dini comparison bridge
remain analytic/formal obligations. No general regularity breakthrough is claimed.

checks establish neither a missing analytic hypothesis nor novelty.
