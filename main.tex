\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Conditional result. Not a Clay Millennium claim.} Nothing in this paper or in the Lean development proves global regularity for the 3D Navier--Stokes equations. Every regularity statement is an implication from named hypotheses that are \emph{open} or classical-but-unformalized. The periodic Clay~(B) reduction is \lean{clay_reduces_to_direction_curvature_bound} in \texttt{MainTheorem.lean}: assuming \lean{PeriodicBKMContinuation}~$\nu$ for every $\nu>0$, the hypothesis \lean{DirectionCurvatureBound}~$\nu$ for every $\nu>0$ implies \lean{Clay_B_exact}. Here \lean{DirectionCurvatureBound} is an abbreviation for \lean{UniformVorticityEnvelopeBound}, an \emph{assumed} uniform ceiling on $\|\omega(t)\|_\infty$ along periodic smooth solutions (not a differential inequality); it is open. The converse is not proved.

We present in full both (1)~the complete original geometric and physical framework of the \textbf{Frohmanian Symplectic Tether} $\Tf$ (preserving without omission all original definitions, physical motivations, conjectured geometric--analytic bridges, and extensions from \emph{Frohmanian\_Tether\_Final\_Submission\_v8}), and (2)~its kernel-verified conditional formalization in Lean~4 (repository \texttt{NS\_Millennium\_Proof}: the in-build axiom audit reports \textbf{682 compiled theorem constants}, \textbf{no \texttt{sorry}, and only the standard axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}}).

Let $M(t)=\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}$ denote the vorticity sup-norm. The solution class of Fefferman's periodic statements (B) and (D) is a classical periodic smooth solution of the unforced equations (\lean{IsPeriodicSmoothSolution} with force $0$): joint smoothness on $[0,\infty)\times\R^3$, the one-sided equation \lean{navier_stokes_eq_Ici}, divergence free, the initial condition, and $\mathbb{Z}^3$ periodicity. The predicates \lean{NS_PDE} and \lean{IsPhysicalNSSolution} are not that class. Mathlib's \lean{fderiv} is $0$ off differentiability, so those predicates admit a steady field $U$ with a nowhere-differentiable Takagi component. $U$ is not a classical solution, and it is not a witness for Clay~(D). Clay~(D) remains open: one smooth periodic initial field for which no smooth solution exists. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} (\lean{classical_vorticity_ceiling}) is only the comparison ceiling. Along every classical periodic smooth solution of the unforced equations, if $M(s)$ is differentiable for every $s\ge0$ and $M'(s)\le C\,M(s)^2-\kappa''\,M(s)^3$ for constants $C>0$ and $\kappa''>0$, then $M(t)\le\max\bigl(M(0),C/\kappa''\bigr)$ for every $t\ge0$. The differential inequality is an assumption. The lemmas \lean{comparison_ode_stability} and \lean{riccati_ceiling_of_vorticity_di} are the scalar comparison. They do not produce a solution. Proposition~\ref{prop:classical-zero} records one classical solution: the zero velocity and the zero pressure. For every real viscosity $\nu$, the zero velocity and the zero pressure form a classical periodic smooth solution of the unforced equations with zero initial velocity. That one field does not prove \lean{FrohmanianTetherRate}~$0$~$\nu$, which quantifies over every classical solution from zero initial data and is open. \lean{frohmanian_tether_theorem} assumes the classical rate for every datum. The rate is open, so the assembled statement is conditional. It is not a proof of Clay~(B).

The weak predicate \lean{IsPhysicalNSSolution} remains an internal energy and time-differentiability condition. It excludes the time-step field (\lean{stepField_not_physical}). On that weak class, \lean{FrohmanianTetherBound}~$0$~$\nu$ holds (\lean{frohmanianTetherBound_zero}), and \lean{conditional_regularity_of_open_hyps} is instantiated at the rest state (\lean{conditional_regularity_nonvacuous_at_zero}). Those facts are not Clay~(B). The classical rate \lean{FrohmanianTetherRate} is not proved at zero. \section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The incompressible Navier--Stokes equations on $\T=\R^3/\mathbb{Z}^3$ (or on $\R^3$ with finite energy) are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:NS}
\partial_t u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u,\qquad \nabla\cdot u=0,\qquad u(0)=u_0,
\end{equation}
with $\nu>0$. Taking the curl yields the vorticity transport equation for $\omega=\nabla\times u$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vort}
\partial_t\omega+(u\cdot\nabla)\omega=(\omega\cdot\nabla)u+\nu\Delta\omega .
\end{equation}
By the Beale--Kato--Majda theorem \cite{BKM1984}, a smooth solution can be continued past $T$ whenever $\int_0^T\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}\,dt<\infty$. A uniform ceiling on $M(t)=\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}$ therefore implies global regularity. Obtaining such a bound for arbitrary large smooth data is the core difficulty of the Clay Millennium Problem \cite{Fefferman2006}: the vortex-stretching term $(\omega\cdot\nabla)u$ is supercritical with respect to the natural parabolic scaling
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaling}
u_\lambda(x,t)=\lambda u(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),\qquad \omega_\lambda(x,t)=\lambda^2\omega(\lambda x,\lambda^2 t),
\end{equation}
which leaves \eqref{eq:NS} invariant at fixed $\nu$.

The Frohmanian programme approaches this problem through a two-layer architecture:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Geometric layer (\texttt{ArnoldGeometric.lean}, \texttt{SymplecticTether.lean}, \texttt{Uniqueness.lean}, \texttt{ForMathlib/Projection.lean}):} Starting from Arnold's coadjoint orbit of volume-preserving diffeomorphisms \cite{Arnold1966,ArnoldKhesin1998}, we introduce the \textbf{Frohmanian Symplectic Tether} $\Tf$—realized concretely via the $L^2$-projected quadratic kernel \lean{TetherKernel} with universal Calderón--Zygmund coupling $\kappa=\CZ(3)=3/2$. By projecting functional derivatives orthogonally to the velocity field $u=\delta H/\delta\omega$ via the Gram--Schmidt projector $\Pi_u$ (\lean{Pi_u}), the tether satisfies exact degeneracy $B(F,H)=0$ on the kinetic-energy Hamiltonian $H$ (\lean{tetherKernel_degenerates_on_kinetic_energy}), leaving the reversible Euler vector field unmodified while supplying controllable negative quadratic feedback $B(F,F)=-\kappa\int|\omega|^2|\Pi_u\delta F/\delta\omega|^2$ (\lean{ProducesControllableNegativeFeedback}).
\item \textbf{Analytic layer (\texttt{NS\_Equations.lean}, \texttt{TetheredLyapunov.lean}, \texttt{IndependentMajorant.lean}, \texttt{CubicSourceCertificate.lean}, \texttt{AnalyticPipeline.lean}, \texttt{ClosureBlueprint.lean}, \texttt{ConditionalRegularity.lean}, \texttt{ClayStatements.lean}):} Using the quartic Lyapunov functional $S_\varepsilon(t)$ weighted by the transported enstrophy-accumulation scalar $\varphi$ (\lean{LyapunovS}, \lean{IsEnstrophyAccumulation}), the programme conjectures the cubic tether damping rate $M'(t)\le \CZ M(t)^2-\kappa'' M(t)^3$ along classical periodic smooth solutions (\lean{FrohmanianTetherRate}). The comparison ODE $y'=Cy^2-\kappa''y^3$ (\lean{comparison_ode_stability}, \lean{comparison_majorant_global_bound}) yields the ceiling $M(t)\le\max(M(0),\CZ/\kappa'')$ when that inequality is assumed (\lean{classical_vorticity_ceiling}, \lean{riccati_ceiling_of_vorticity_di}). The inequality is open. Global regularity is not closed.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{theorem}[Comparison ceiling on a classical periodic smooth solution; \lean{classical_vorticity_ceiling}]\label{thm:main}
Along every classical periodic smooth solution of the unforced equations, if $M(s)$ is differentiable for every $s\ge0$ and $M'(s)\le C\,M(s)^2-\kappa''\,M(s)^3$ for constants $C>0$ and $\kappa''>0$, then $M(t)\le\max\bigl(M(0),C/\kappa''\bigr)$ for every $t\ge0$.
\end{theorem}
The classical solution is \lean{IsPeriodicSmoothSolution} with force $0$. The inequality on $M$ is assumed. The theorem does not assert that a solution exists, and it does not prove Clay~(B) or Clay~(D).

\begin{proposition}[The zero field is one classical periodic smooth solution; \lean{zero_isPeriodicSmoothSolution}]\label{prop:classical-zero}
For every real viscosity $\nu$, the zero velocity and the zero pressure form a classical periodic smooth solution of the unforced equations with zero initial velocity.
\end{proposition}
This is the zero datum only. Clay~(B) asks for a smooth solution from every smooth divergence-free periodic field, and that statement stays open. The steady Takagi field $U$ satisfies \lean{NS_PDE} and \lean{IsPhysicalNSSolution} and has unbounded vorticity, so it refutes the weak-class bound \lean{FrohmanianTetherBound}. $U$ is not a classical solution. Clay~(D) stays open, and $U$ is not a Clay~(D) witness. \lean{FrohmanianTetherRate} quantifies over classical periodic smooth solutions. It is open, including at zero initial data.

\paragraph{Scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.} Theorem~\ref{thm:main} assumes a classical periodic smooth solution of the unforced equations and assumes the cubic differential inequality for its vorticity sup-norm. \lean{comparison_ode_stability} then gives the ceiling. No derivation of the inequality from \eqref{eq:vort} is claimed. The constants $C,\kappa''$ are those in the assumption. They are not shown to be universal. The separate statement \lean{conditional_regularity_of_open_hyps} still assumes a time-uniform bound on the weak class \lean{NS_PDE} plus \lean{IsPhysicalNSSolution}. That bound is open for nonzero data. It is not Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. In the Lean development the type \lean{T3} is \texttt{EuclideanSpace}~$\mathbb{R}$~\texttt{(Fin 3)}. Periodicity is the predicate \lean{IsZ3Periodic}, not a change of the underlying space to the torus. A nonzero periodic field need not have finite energy on $\mathbb{R}^3$, so it need not lie in \lean{IsPhysicalNSSolution}. The periodic Clay~(B) reduction remains \lean{clay_reduces_to_direction_curvature_bound}, described in the abstract, and it is still conditional.

\section{Setting and the Physical Solution Class}\label{sec:setting}

Write $M(t)=\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}$ (Lean: \lean{vorticity_sup_norm (vorticity (u t))}). In the Lean formalization, the pointwise Navier--Stokes equation is \lean{NS_PDE u p ν}. Because Mathlib's \lean{deriv} returns $0$ at points where a function of time is not differentiable, a spatially smooth field with jump discontinuities in time would trivially satisfy \lean{NS_PDE} unless time-differentiability and energy control are enforced.

We therefore define the physical solution class \lean{IsPhysicalNSSolution} in \lean{NS_Equations.lean}:
\leanfile{IsPhysicalNSSolution}
A time-dependent velocity field $u$ belongs to \lean{IsPhysicalNSSolution} if and only if for all $t\ge0$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $s\mapsto u(s,x)$ is pointwise differentiable at $t$ for every $x\in\R^3$;
\item $x\mapsto u(t,x)$ is continuous and has finite kinetic energy $\int_{\R^3}\|u(t,x)\|^2\,dx<\infty$;
\item $u$ obeys the classical Leray $L^2$ energy inequality $\int_{\R^3}\|u(t,x)\|^2\,dx\le\int_{\R^3}\|u(0,x)\|^2\,dx$;
\item $u(t)=u(0)$ for $t\le0$ (initial-value convention on $(-\infty,0]$).
\end{enumerate}

This predicate is not the solution class of Clay~(B) or Clay~(D). Those statements use \lean{IsPeriodicSmoothSolution}: joint \lean{ContDiffOn} smoothness of velocity and pressure on $[0,\infty)\times\R^3$, the one-sided equation \lean{navier_stokes_eq_Ici}, the initial condition, and \lean{IsZ3Periodic}. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Proposition~\ref{prop:classical-zero} use that class with force $0$. The definition is in \lean{NS_Equations.lean}.
\leanfile{IsPeriodicSmoothSolution}

The classical PDE inputs enter as explicit, transparent hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}
\item Kato local existence on physical solutions (\lean{KatoLocalWitness}, carrying \lean{phys : IsPhysicalNSSolution u}) \cite{Kato1984,Temam1995};
\item Beale--Kato--Majda continuation and parabolic smoothing (\lean{bkm_regularity_pipeline}) \cite{BKM1984,ConstantinFoias1988};
\item Energy-Grönwall uniqueness of smooth physical solutions (\lean{uniqueness_of_existence_times_of_energy_gronwall}) \cite{Temam1995}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Geometric Layer: The Frohmanian Symplectic Tether}\label{sec:geom}

\subsection{The Classical Arnold Lie--Poisson Bracket}
On the coadjoint orbit $\mathcal O$ of the group $\mathrm{SDiff}(\T)$ of volume-preserving diffeomorphisms, the classical Kirillov--Kostant--Souriau / Arnold Lie--Poisson bracket \cite{Arnold1966,ArnoldKhesin1998} is
\[
\{F,G\}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\omega)=\int_{\T}\omega\cdot\Bigl(\frac{\delta F}{\delta\omega}\times\frac{\delta G}{\delta\omega}\Bigr)\,dV .
\]
With kinetic-energy Hamiltonian $H(\omega)=\tfrac12\int|u|^2\,dV$ (where $\delta H/\delta\omega=u=\mathrm{BS}(\omega)$ is the Biot--Savart velocity reconstruction, formalized in \lean{ArnoldGeometric.lean}), the Hamiltonian vector field of $\{F,H\}_{\mathrm{cl}}$ reproduces the ideal 3D Euler vorticity equation ($\nu=0$ in \eqref{eq:vort}; Lean: \lean{classical_bracket_reproduces_Euler}).

\subsection{Route A: The Projected Tether Kernel and Degeneracy}
\begin{definition}[Frohmanian tether kernel; Lean \lean{TetherKernel}, \lean{Pi_u}]\label{def:tether}
For a velocity field $u=\mathrm{BS}(\omega)$ with $\|u\|_{L^2}\neq0$, let $\Pi_u=P^\perp_u$ be the $L^2$-orthogonal Gram--Schmidt projection onto the complement of $\operatorname{span}\{u\}$:
\[
\Pi_u v=v-\frac{\langle v,u\rangle_{L^2}}{\|u\|_{L^2}^2}\,u
\]
(Lean: \lean{ForMathlib.Pi_u}, with $\Pi_u v=v$ when $\|u\|_{L^2}=0$). With universal 3D Calderón--Zygmund constant $\kappa=\CZ(3)=\frac{3}{8\pi}\cdot 4\pi=\frac{3}{2}$ (Lean: \lean{kappa_eq_three_halves} in \lean{ForMathlib/NS/Tether.lean}), define the Frohmanian tether kernel
\[
B(F,G)(\omega)=-\kappa\int_{\T}|\omega(x)|^2\,\Bigl\langle \Pi_u\Bigl(\frac{\delta F}{\delta\omega}\Bigr)(x),\,\Pi_u\Bigl(\frac{\delta G}{\delta\omega}\Bigr)(x)\Bigr\rangle\,dV .
\]
The full tethered bracket is $\{F,G\}_{\Tf}=\{F,G\}_{\mathrm{cl}}+B(F,G)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Strict Degeneracy on Kinetic Energy; proved, \lean{tetherKernel_degenerates_on_kinetic_energy}, \lean{tetherKernel_C2}]\label{prop:degen}
Whenever $\delta H/\delta\omega=u$, $\nabla\cdot u=0$, and $u\in L^2$, one has $\Pi_u u=0$ (\lean{projection_orthogonal_to_u}) and hence
\[
B(F,H)(\omega)=0\qquad\text{for every functional }F.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $\|u\|_{L^2}\ne0$, $\Pi_u u=u-(\langle u,u\rangle_{L^2}/\|u\|_{L^2}^2)u=0$, so the projected factor $\Pi_u(\delta H/\delta\omega)$ vanishes pointwise. If $\|u\|_{L^2}=0$, $u=0$ a.e.\ and $\Pi_u 0=0$ (\lean{Pi_u_zero}). Thus $\{F,H\}_{\Tf}=\{F,H\}_{\mathrm{cl}}$ for all $F$: the reversible Euler vector field generated by $H$ is preserved unmodified.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Symmetry vs.\ Antisymmetry and Dual Uniqueness Theorems}
As an explicit bilinear integral kernel, $B(F,G)$ in Definition~\ref{def:tether} is \emph{symmetric} in $(F,G)$ (\lean{tetherKernel_comm}, \lean{tetherKernel_symmetric}), acting as a weighted, $L^2$-projected metric pairing of metriplectic type \cite{Morrison1986}. In \lean{Uniqueness.lean}, we formalize and prove \textbf{both} uniqueness characterizations of \lean{TetherKernel}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Symmetric metric-tether uniqueness (\lean{uniqueness_of_minimal_symmetric_tether}):} Among all \emph{symmetric} bilinear kernels $B'(\omega,F,G)=B'(\omega,G,F)$ satisfying coadjoint invariance (C1), kinetic-energy degeneracy (C2), controllable negative feedback (C3), lowest-order canonical density (\lean{IsLowestOrderTerm}), and quadratic saturation (\lean{SaturatesTetherQuadratic}), $B'$ is uniquely equal to \lean{TetherKernel}. Moreover, \lean{TetherKernel} itself satisfies the symmetry hypothesis (\lean{tetherKernel_symmetric}).
\item \textbf{Antisymmetric uniqueness (\lean{uniqueness_of_minimal_tether}):} Preserves the original antisymmetric binder $\forall\omega F G,\ B'(\omega,F,G)=-B'(\omega,G,F)$ alongside (C1)--(C3), \lean{IsLowestOrderTerm}, and \lean{SaturatesTetherQuadratic}, proving $B'=\text{\lean{TetherKernel}}$ on the locus where those hypotheses hold.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Coadjoint Invariance and the 9-Term Reduced Jacobi Identity}
In \lean{SymplecticTether.lean}, coadjoint invariance and the reduced Jacobi identity are formalized at two levels:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Conditional coadjoint invariance:} \lean{tether_coadjoint_invariance} and \lean{tetherKernel_C1} verify \lean{InvariantUnderCoadjointAction TetherKernel} only \emph{conditionally} on \lean{SDiffActionHyp} and \lean{TetherIntegralPreserved}, for an \emph{abstract} group acting on the orbit type (a Lean \texttt{MulAction}); it is not constructed as the volume-preserving diffeomorphism group of $\T$, so this is \emph{not} a proof of invariance under the actual $\mathrm{SDiff}(\T)$ coadjoint action. (Before this version \lean{CoadjointAction} was the identity map and the invariance was trivial.) We also define \lean{ConjecturedSDiffPushforwardInvariance} and prove \lean{tether_coadjoint_invariance_of_integral_eq}: volume-preserving push-forward equivariance of the projected integrand, which is assumed, yields coadjoint invariance of \lean{TetherKernel}.
\item \textbf{9-Term Reduced Jacobi Identity (\lean{tethered_jacobi_identity}):} Expands the cyclic Jacobiator of $\{F,G\}_{\Tf}=\{F,G\}_{\mathrm{cl}}+B(F,G)$ into its 9 constituent terms (3 pure classical Euler Lie--Poisson terms + 6 cross/tether terms grouped into cyclic families A, B, C) and proves that the full Jacobiator vanishes under the explicit Chevalley--Eilenberg cocycle cancellation hypothesis.
\end{itemize}

\section{Local-Maximum Lemmas, the Cubic Comparison Ceiling, and Non-Vacuity}\label{sec:cubic}

\subsection{Local-Maximum Stretching and Transport Lemmas}
\begin{lemma}[Stretching at a spatial maximum; proved, \lean{stretching_inner_le_at_max}]\label{lem:localmax}
Let $x$ satisfy $\|\omega(x)\|=\|\omega\|_{L^\infty}$ and suppose $\|\nabla u(x)\|\le\CZ\|\omega\|_{L^\infty}$ (\lean{hCZ}). Then
\[
\langle\omega(x),(\omega\cdot\nabla)u(x)\rangle\le\CZ\,\|\omega\|_{L^\infty}^3 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Transport and viscosity at a spatial maximum; proved, \lean{time_pairing_le_stretching_at_max}]\label{lem:transport}
At a spatial maximum where the convective transport pairing $\langle\omega,(u\cdot\nabla)\omega\rangle(x)$ vanishes and $\langle\omega,\Delta\omega\rangle(x)\le0$, along any solution of \eqref{eq:vort} with $\nu\ge0$,
\[
\langle\omega,\partial_t\omega\rangle(x)\le\langle\omega,(\omega\cdot\nabla)u\rangle(x).
\]
\end{lemma}

\subsection{The Quartic Lyapunov Vehicle and Cubic Comparison ODE}
Let $\varphi$ solve the linear transport equation (\lean{IsEnstrophyAccumulation})
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi}
\partial_t\varphi+u\cdot\nabla\varphi=|\omega|^2,
\end{equation}
and for mollified vorticity $\omega_\varepsilon$ define the tethered quartic Lyapunov functional (\lean{LyapunovS})
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S}
